## 5. Source role and temporal fitness

The build plan names several source families. Their role separation is more
important than the mere fact that they are public.

| Source family in the plan | Proposed role | Must not become |
|---|---|---|
| National Park Service | Public historic-trail and visitor interpretation | Complete history, cultural authority, grave/archaeology locator, vulnerable-feature inventory, or current safety guarantee |
| Kansas Geological Survey / GeoKansas | Geological and historical-scientific interpretation | Present groundwater, well, potability, health, flood, or legal authority |
| Marshall County public website | Civic and administrative orientation | Title, property, cultural, visitor-condition, emergency, or public-safety authority beyond source scope |
| Kansas Department of Agriculture | County statistical and floodplain-administrator routing context | Farm/owner profile, property flood determination, permit/insurance advice, or current emergency source |
| U.S. Geological Survey monitoring page | Monitoring-location identity and, after admission, time-bounded observations | Crossing safety, flood warning, recreation advice, or complete river truth |
| Future site-manager source | Current public-site routing and approved visitor context | Permission to expose fragile resources or certify conditions beyond its actual scope |
| Future Indigenous, Tribal, Nation, or community-authoritative source | Bounded cultural interpretation under an appropriate relationship and review process | A token citation used to claim universal, complete, or transferable cultural authority |
| AI-generated narrative | Bounded explanation after evidence and policy checks | Evidence, public-history authority, cultural authority, safety advice, review approval, or release authority |

### Temporal rules

1. Preserve the source's own observation, publication, update, retrieval,
   effective, rescission, and validity times when material.
2. A dated planning observation does not answer a current visitor, river, flood,
   property, groundwater, or safety question.
3. Historical scientific material must be labeled by source character and
   reference period.
4. A current monitoring page does not make every interpretation current,
   complete, or action-guiding.
5. Missing or unclear freshness for consequential claims produces `ABSTAIN`,
   `DENY`, or `ERROR`, not a guessed current answer.
6. Corrections, supersessions, removals, and changed cultural or site-manager
   guidance must remain visible when a source or released KFM object changes.

### Rights and derivative-display rules

Public page access is not proof of permission to cache, transform, redistribute,
map, export, summarize, or combine every underlying element. Before any public
artifact, the responsible lane must establish rights, attribution, permitted
derivatives, geometry precision, sensitive-detail handling, and correction
obligations.

## 6. Fragile heritage, burial, archaeology, and inscriptions

### Fragile-heritage boundary

The build plan treats public trail places, swales, inscriptions, and physical
traces as a protection problem, not a discovery-product opportunity. A future
surface should prefer generalized public-site context and reviewed visitor
routing rather than feature-level condition, location, ranking, or extraction
assistance.

### Burial boundary

A public historical statement that a death or burial occurred does not
authorize KFM to map, infer, predict, rank, or crowdsource possible burial
locations. Unknown location remains unknown. Terrain, proximity, historic camp
patterns, archival hints, or model inference must not be used to create a
public predictive-search surface.

### Archaeology and artifacts

Do not turn public interpretation into:

- artifact or archaeological prospecting;
- buried-object probability mapping;
- off-trail search recommendations;
- feature-condition inventories that increase vulnerability;
- inscription-rubbing, collection, removal, or damage assistance;
- joins that reconstruct withheld locations from trails, terrain, historic
  camps, parcels, photographs, or user submissions.

### Cross-layer reconstruction

Generalized public layers must be reviewed in composition. Multiple
individually safe layers must not reconstruct graves, archaeological leads,
fragile traces, restricted access, or site vulnerabilities through overlay,
proximity, labels, search, export, routing, or generated explanation.

### Safe first-slice posture

The safest first slice is static, generalized, evidence-linked public context
with visible protection notices. Detailed geometry, user-generated site
reports, predictive analysis, and extraction-oriented search remain excluded.

## 7. Interpretation authority and cultural gaps

### Source-role honesty

The build plan's checked sources support bounded federal, state, county,
historic-trail, and geological interpretations. They do not by themselves
establish a complete account of the corridor's histories, cultural meanings,
sovereignty, relationships, or community priorities.

### Appropriate authority

A future expansion involving Indigenous peoples, Tribal Nations, cultural use,
sacred relationships, place names, displacement, sovereignty, or community
knowledge requires an appropriate evidence and review pathway. The exact
authority, consultation, consent, attribution, access, and correction process
is **NEEDS VERIFICATION** and cannot be invented by this README or by AI.

### Required abstention

Until an appropriate pathway is established, the system should abstain from:

- calling an emigrant-trail interpretation the complete history of the place;
- generating missing cultural narratives from adjacent public prose;
- assigning cultural meaning or affiliation;
- presenting generalized model knowledge as community-authorized;
- using a single source to flatten differing Nations, communities, eras, or
  viewpoints;
- exposing sensitive cultural knowledge or locations.

### Representation notice

A future public interface should show a visible interpretation-authority notice
that distinguishes:

- the source roles currently admitted;
- the claims those sources can support;
- the perspectives and authorities not yet represented;
- the reason for abstention or deferred expansion;
- the correction and review route for harmful, incomplete, or superseded
  representation.

## 8. Big Blue River, floodplain, and public safety

### Static river context

A future public-safe slice may explain the Big Blue River as landscape and
historic-crossing context using admitted, time-bounded evidence. That does not
make KFM a live river, flood, crossing, recreation, weather, or emergency
service.

### Current-condition boundary

KFM must not determine whether it is safe to:

- cross, wade, paddle, fish, hike, camp, or approach the river;
- use a historic route or public site today;
- rely on a gauge value without qualified interpretation;
- travel during flooding, high water, severe weather, closure, or emergency
  conditions.

Current action questions require responsible, current authority. A future
governed surface may provide clearly labeled routing metadata, but it must not
turn a link or stale observation into a KFM recommendation.

### Floodplain and property boundary

Floodplain participation, maps, and administrative sources can explain source
roles and public processes. They do not authorize parcel-specific flood,
insurance, permit, building, legal, title, or value conclusions.

### Monitoring-source boundary

A monitoring station can establish source identity and time-bounded
observations after admission. It does not establish:

- full river conditions beyond the measurement scope;
- safe crossing or recreation;
- future conditions;
- flood-warning authority;
- property impact;
- ecological health;
- causation or liability.

## 9. Agriculture, historical science, property, ecology, and infrastructure

### Agriculture

County-scale agricultural statistics are aggregate planning context. Do not
join them to parcel, owner, household, operator, water-use, tax, complaint, or
environmental records to infer individual conduct, responsibility, liability,
or cultural impact.

### Historical science

The build plan describes KGS geology and groundwater material as historical or
scientific context. Any future card must preserve source date, method, scale,
limitations, and fitness. It must not answer current questions about:

- private wells or water supply;
- drinking-water safety or health;
- pumping legality or water rights;
- current groundwater levels at a specific property;
- flood or river conditions;
- engineering suitability.

### Property and people

Do not turn county, flood, trail, or public GIS sources into:

- title or ownership determinations;
- permission-to-enter advice;
- living-person or household profiles;
- parcel valuation or insurance conclusions;
- private-land conflict narratives;
- implied liability or blame.

### Ecology

General river-corridor or landscape learning does not authorize exact
species, nest, roost, rare-habitat, management, or occurrence detail. Heritage
and ecology layers must also be reviewed together so one does not reveal the
other's withheld locations.

### Infrastructure

Exact vulnerability-sensitive bridge, road, utility, communications, emergency,
or operational facility detail should not be exposed merely because it is
publicly visible elsewhere or spatially joinable. Minimum necessary detail and
current policy govern any future display.

## 17. Maintenance, migration, correction, and rollback

### Maintenance triggers

Revisit this README when:

- the sibling plan changes materially;
- Focus Mode placement or naming authority changes;
- source admission, rights, heritage, cultural-authority, hydrology,
  property, ecology, or sensitivity review establishes new facts;
- a Marshall County contract, schema, fixture, validator, API, UI, or release
  object is implemented and verified;
- public-safe detail or generalization policy changes;
- a correction, withdrawal, or supersession affects a cited county claim;
- the county index is recomputed against current repository state.

### Migration posture

The current path is a compatibility lane. Proposed ADR-0027 describes a plural,
kebab-case control plane but remains proposed. Do not move or copy this README
into a second writable tree until an adopted decision, migration plan,
consumer/link inventory, write freeze, history-preserving move, compatibility
strategy, validation, and rollback boundary are established.

A migration must not leave two independently editable Marshall County
authorities.

### Correction posture

A documentation correction should preserve the old claim's lineage where
material and identify the evidence that changed. A public correction is a
separate governed action and may require API, map, cache, export, search, AI,
site-manager, preservation, or community propagation beyond a Markdown edit.

### Withdrawal and harm response

Future released content should be withdrawable when it:

- exposes or helps reconstruct sensitive heritage or cultural information;
- misstates source or cultural authority;
- creates unsafe river, flood, property, or visitor reliance;
- violates rights or attribution;
- becomes stale, superseded, or materially misleading.

### Rollback

Before merge, rollback is to close or abandon the draft pull request and feature
branch. After merge, use a transparent revert or bounded forward fix against
the actual merged commit; do not rewrite shared history.

If a future released Marshall County artifact is implicated, a Git revert alone
may be insufficient. Correction, withdrawal, cache invalidation, alias changes,
supersession, reviewer notification, and release rollback must follow the
applicable KFM controls.
