## 5. Geology, scientific-locality, access, and property boundaries

### 5.1 Geology is evidence-bearing context, not permission

A future Ottawa map may explain a reviewed geologic interpretation of Rock City
and related formations. It must keep separate:

- scientific interpretation from visitor/operator rules;
- public visibility from permission to enter or collect;
- public coordinates from approved scientific-locality precision;
- geologic description from archaeological or cultural sensitivity;
- map geometry from property/title truth.

A source describing a place does not establish every permitted activity at that
place. KFM should never infer permission from discoverability.

### 5.2 Scientific-locality restraint

Before exposing locality precision, review whether the location or nearby features
could support unauthorized collecting, excavation, vandalism, archaeological
exposure, trespass, or resource damage. Where the answer is unclear, generalize,
withhold, quarantine, or deny.

### 5.3 Access and property restraint

A future public surface should not answer questions such as:

- "Can I collect a concretion here?"
- "Can I climb this feature?"
- "Can I enter from this road or parcel?"
- "Where is the property line?"
- "Does this map prove public access or an easement?"
- "Who owns this feature?"

unless a separately governed, current, fit-for-purpose authority explicitly
supports the exact answer. Even then, KFM should present the source role and
limitations rather than convert a map into a legal determination.

## 6. Hydrology, weather, currentness, and public-safety boundaries

### 6.1 Monitoring is not warning authority

A river observation can support a statement about a measurement at an identified
time and station when admitted and released. It does not independently support:

- a flood warning;
- a crossing-safety recommendation;
- a boating or swimming recommendation;
- a water-quality conclusion;
- drinking-water safety;
- a road-closure determination;
- an emergency-protective action.

Those questions require the appropriate current authority and fitness-for-use
review.

### 6.2 Currentness must be visible

Every time-sensitive future object should distinguish, where material:

- observation time;
- source update or issue time;
- retrieval time;
- KFM processing time;
- release time;
- supersession or correction time.

A stale source may remain useful as historical evidence while being unfit for a
current operational claim.

### 6.3 Official-current redirect posture

When the user needs current weather, flood, road, emergency, visitor, fishing,
boating, or other safety-sensitive status, a future Focus surface should route or
refer to the responsible current authority instead of synthesizing an answer from
stale or merely adjacent evidence.
