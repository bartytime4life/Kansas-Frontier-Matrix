## 5. Source role, rights, and temporal fitness

The build plan lists candidate public sources. This README does not recheck,
admit, activate, or certify any of them. A source becomes usable only after the
appropriate KFM source record establishes identity, authority role, rights,
access, cadence, spatial and temporal scope, sensitivity, permitted use,
limitations, and correction behavior.

### Proposed source-role routing matrix

| Candidate source family named by the plan | What it may help support after admission | What it cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| County and city official sources | Current civic routing, public facilities, ordinances, notices, and locally authored context within source scope | Military authority, sovereign cultural authority, title, private facts, or comprehensive historical truth |
| Public Army, garrison, DoD, or military-history sources | Official public military history and approved public institutional context | Permission to expose operational details, all historical perspectives, or security-sensitive information |
| Nation-authored or tribally governed sources | Identity, history, governance, language, cultural framing, consent, and sensitivity decisions within stated authority | A blanket release of precise cultural or sacred locations, or endorsement beyond the source scope |
| Kansas Historical Society, Kansas Memory, archives, libraries, maps, and local-history collections | Historical documents, images, maps, finding aids, and public-history context | Automatic factual certainty, current conditions, rights clearance for every asset, or absence of bias and omission |
| National Archives and other federal records | Federal archival context and source lineage | Current policy, individual privacy clearance, or a complete account without contextual review |
| USGS and hydrologic sources | Observed or modeled water and geographic context within the product definition | Regulatory flood status, emergency direction, or legal and engineering advice |
| FEMA and regulatory flood products | Regulatory or program-specific flood information within the relevant edition and jurisdiction | Current river observation, emergency prediction, property-specific insurance or legal determination |
| Federal and state corrections sources | Public institutional records, official statistics, and generalized public context | Individual legal status, private records, security details, active-case interpretation, or complete historical narrative |
| VA or public health-institution sources | Public institutional and program context | Patient, veteran, family, health, disability, or service-record disclosure |
| Transportation and planning sources | Time-bounded roads, networks, programs, and aggregate planning context | Present access, unrestricted travel, private-property permission, or historical certainty without lineage |

### Source-role anti-collapse rules

Never silently treat these as interchangeable:

- observation, forecast, model, regulatory map, interpretation, and historical
  reconstruction;
- official public record, archival document, public-history narrative, and
  generated summary;
- federal, state, county, city, sovereign Nation, institutional, scholarly,
  community, and aggregator authority;
- facility location, public-history context, operational infrastructure, and
  vulnerability information;
- aggregate corrections statistics, facility history, individual record, and
  legal status;
- assessor or tax information, property interest, ownership, and legal title;
- historical route hypothesis, mapped corridor, current road access, and lawful
  entry.

### Time fields that must remain distinct

| Time concept | Why it matters in this lane |
|---|---|
| Event or valid time | When the described historical, institutional, hydrologic, or planning condition applied |
| Observation time | When a measurement, status, or source observation was made |
| Source publication or revision time | Which edition or public record version supported the claim |
| Retrieval time | When KFM captured or checked the source |
| Review time | When evidence, rights, sensitivity, and public-use fitness were reviewed |
| Release time | When a governed public-safe object became eligible for public delivery |
| Correction or withdrawal time | When a public interpretation, source state, or released object changed |

A current-status question cannot be answered from a historical source merely
because both refer to the same place. Missing temporal support requires
`ABSTAIN`, `DENY`, or `ERROR` according to the failure, not an undated answer.

### Rights and retention boundary

Public availability does not imply unrestricted reuse. Before committing source
material or derivatives, record the applicable terms, attribution, license,
redaction, retention, redistribution, and withdrawal conditions. When rights are
unclear, preserve only the minimum review metadata needed and keep content out
of public or semi-public paths.

## 6. Military, corrections, detention, and infrastructure safety

### Public-history context is not operational authority

A future public-safe Fort or institution layer may show only the reviewed level
of context needed for the claim. It must not expose or help reconstruct:

- operational plans, readiness, communications, access control, surveillance,
  security zones, current training, protected personnel, or deployment details;
- non-public infrastructure, utilities, internal circulation, vulnerabilities,
  emergency procedures, or security response capabilities;
- combinations of individually public facts whose aggregation creates a harmful
  operational picture.

Public-facing precision should be no greater than the reviewed evidence and use
case require. A visible feature may need generalized geometry, a bounded label,
a non-interactive summary, delayed time, staged access, or complete denial.

### Corrections and detention boundaries

A generalized public institutional card must remain separate from:

- inmate, detainee, resident, patient, employee, contractor, family, visitor, or
  complainant records;
- active cases, release dates, medical or disability information, immigration
  status, allegations, risk scores, disciplinary information, or legal advice;
- facility layouts, routines, staffing, movement, transport, security systems,
  vulnerabilities, or incident-response details;
- generated conclusions drawn from news, social media, incomplete records, or
  a single institutional source.

News or commentary may identify a verification need. It does not establish an
individual's legal status, a facility's current operating condition, or the
truth of an allegation.

### Privacy and aggregation checks

Even aggregate or generalized output requires review for:

- small-cell re-identification;
- temporal or geographic linkage across multiple public datasets;
- household, family, employee, veteran, patient, inmate, or detainee inference;
- location trails, routines, or sensitive institutional relationships;
- search, export, tile, cache, screenshot, or AI paths that reveal more than the
  ordinary UI intends.

### Current safety and operations

KFM planning documentation is not a source for current military, corrections,
detention, law-enforcement, road, flood, health, or emergency decisions. A user
seeking current operational guidance must be routed to the responsible current
authority. When routing cannot be established safely, the correct outcome is
`ABSTAIN` or `DENY` rather than an inferred answer.

## 7. River, history, culture, property, and privacy boundaries

### Missouri River and flood context

A river or floodplain surface must preserve source role and jurisdiction:

- observed water conditions are not forecasts;
- forecasts are not regulatory flood maps;
- regulatory maps are not current emergency notices;
- derived visualizations are not engineering or insurance determinations;
- a historic channel or crossing is not present-day access;
- a map extent or symbol does not prove exact location, boundary, or legal effect.

Public UI should make data age, product role, spatial resolution, uncertainty,
and correction state visible. It must not provide current evacuation, boating,
navigation, flood, insurance, property, or emergency guidance unless a separate
current governed capability supports that use.

### Territorial and early-city claims

The build plan uses phrases such as “first city,” territorial politics, and the
Leavenworth Constitution as planning themes. These are consequential historical
claims and require:

- precise claim wording and time scope;
- multiple source roles where perspectives differ;
- explicit distinction between primary record, later interpretation, and public
  memory;
- visible uncertainty, conflict, omission, and correction paths;
- no implication that territorial or settlement narratives erase earlier or
  continuing Indigenous presence, governance, displacement, treaty, removal,
  or community history.

A county Focus Mode must not flatten conflict into a single official story or
use generated prose as the reconciliation mechanism.

### Indigenous, cultural, burial, and archaeological context

Before representing identity, governance, language, cultural history, sacred
places, burials, archaeology, traditional knowledge, or culturally restricted
material:

- identify the appropriate Nation-authored or community authority;
- establish rights, consent, sensitivity, representation scope, and review;
- avoid treating county, state, federal, tourism, scholarly, map, or generated
  sources as substitutes for sovereign or cultural authority;
- generalize, redact, delay, stage, or deny exact locations and reconstructive
  clues when exposure could cause harm;
- preserve the right to correct, narrow, withdraw, or supersede representation.

### Property, parcel, land, and access

Parcel, tax, assessor, zoning, planning, map, or address data must not be treated
as conclusive title, ownership, access permission, boundary, easement, or legal
status. Public layers should avoid private-address and household profiling, and
historical routes should not imply lawful current entry.

### Genealogy and living people

Military, corrections, detention, veteran, cemetery, household, and archival
sources can intersect with living people and families. A public historical use
must not expose or infer sensitive personal information. Living-person data,
private family relationships, health information, service records, legal status,
and exact residence require explicit authority and fail-closed review.

| `ANSWER` | Released, in-scope evidence supports the bounded claim; policy and citation checks allow the response | Evidence references, citations, source roles, time and spatial scope, limitations, policy state, release and correction context |
| `ABSTAIN` | Evidence is unresolved, stale, conflicted, too broad, or not fit for the requested time or place | Evidence gap, scope limit, stale or conflict reason, and a safe next verification step where available |
| `DENY` | Rights, sensitivity, sovereignty, privacy, security, operational detail, or release state prohibits the response or geometry | Policy-bound reason and only the public-safe explanation that may be disclosed |
| `ERROR` | Schema, resolver, policy, citation, adapter, or runtime execution failed | Stable error code and safe diagnostic without private reasoning, secrets, or protected detail |

“Answer with limits” is not a fifth outcome. It is an `ANSWER` with explicit
scope, limitations, obligations, and withheld-detail metadata.

### Proposed reason-code guidance

The codes below are **documentation proposals**, not accepted contract or policy
enums. Reuse an existing accepted vocabulary when one exists; otherwise review
and version any new code through the owning contract and policy surfaces.

| Proposed code | Intended outcome | Example trigger |
|---|---|---|
| `FOCUS_EVIDENCE_UNRESOLVED` | `ABSTAIN` | Plan claim has no resolvable `EvidenceRef -> EvidenceBundle` chain |
| `FOCUS_SOURCE_ROLE_UNCLEAR` | `ABSTAIN` | Official record, interpretation, regulatory product, or model role is ambiguous |
| `FOCUS_SOURCE_STALE` | `ABSTAIN` | Current-status request is supported only by an outdated source state |
| `FOCUS_SCOPE_TOO_BROAD` | `ABSTAIN` | Question combines incompatible times, institutions, or legal conclusions |
| `FOCUS_RESTRICTED_MILITARY_DETAIL` | `DENY` | Request seeks operational installation, personnel, training, security, or infrastructure detail |
| `FOCUS_RESTRICTED_CORRECTIONS_DETAIL` | `DENY` | Request seeks facility-security, active-operations, vulnerability, or private corrections information |
| `FOCUS_PERSONAL_RECORD_PROTECTED` | `DENY` | Request seeks or infers protected living-person, inmate, detainee, veteran, patient, staff, or family data |
| `FOCUS_SENSITIVE_CULTURAL_LOCATION` | `DENY` | Exact or reconstructive sacred, burial, archaeological, or culturally restricted detail is requested |
| `FOCUS_PROPERTY_NON_DETERMINATION` | `ABSTAIN` or `DENY` | Parcel or assessor data is being used as title, access, or legal proof |
| `FOCUS_LIFECYCLE_PATH_LEAK` | `ERROR` | Public payload points to `RAW`, `WORK`, `QUARANTINE`, restricted, or unreleased stores |
| `FOCUS_RUNTIME_FAILURE` | `ERROR` | Resolver, schema, policy, citation, adapter, or envelope processing fails |

### Representative examples

| Request | Safe bounded result before evidence and policy closure |
|---|---|
| “Why is Fort Leavenworth important to Kansas history?” | `ABSTAIN` until reviewed public evidence supports a precisely scoped answer; then `ANSWER` with citations and public-safety limits |
| “Show restricted installation infrastructure or current security zones.” | `DENY` |
| “Show prison or detention security layouts or routines.” | `DENY` |
| “List private inmate, detainee, staff, veteran, patient, or family records.” | `DENY` |
| “What does this historic map prove about current property access?” | `ABSTAIN`; map context is not title or permission |
| “Is this location safe from flooding right now?” | `ABSTAIN` and route to current responsible authorities when possible |
| “Publish this claim even though its evidence bundle is unresolved.” | `DENY` or `ERROR`, depending on the attempted transition |

### Minimum evidence before a public `ANSWER`

A future public claim should require, in proportion to consequence:

1. a reviewed source identity and authority role;
2. rights, access, attribution, retention, and redistribution posture;
3. explicit spatial and temporal scope;
4. an `EvidenceRef` resolving to admissible evidence;
5. sensitivity, privacy, sovereignty, security, and harmful-precision review;
6. a policy result and required obligations;
7. citation validation and visible limitations;
8. a review record and release decision;
9. correction, withdrawal, and rollback support;
10. a governed API or released artifact path that does not expose internal stores.

Until those conditions exist, the presence of a plan, layer name, map symbol,
schema, test, branch, pull request, or fluent explanation cannot create county
truth or public release state.

## 10. Smallest safe follow-up

The smallest useful next slice is a **no-network, synthetic source-and-safety
proof for one generalized Leavenworth public-context claim**. It should remain
separate from live source activation and public release.

### Proposed bounded outcome

Demonstrate that one candidate claim can travel through a deterministic local
fixture path and return all relevant finite outcomes without exposing military,
corrections, personal, cultural, property, or lifecycle-protected detail.

### Acceptance boundary

- choose one narrowly worded public-context claim from the plan;
- document its expected source roles without asserting that candidate sources
  are admitted;
- provide a synthetic valid evidence packet and deterministic invalid packets;
- prove `ANSWER` only for the valid public-safe fixture;
- prove `ABSTAIN` for unresolved, stale, conflicted, or overbroad support;
- prove `DENY` for restricted military, corrections, personal-record, cultural-
  location, or harmful-precision requests;
- prove `ERROR` for invalid envelope, resolver, policy, or lifecycle-path
  failures;
- keep every fixture synthetic, minimal, rights-safe, no-network, and clearly
  non-production;
- do not add a live connector, model call, public route, release manifest,
  deployment, or publication claim.

- Who is the accountable Leavenworth County Focus Mode owner?
- Which independent reviewers cover military security, corrections and
  detention, privacy, cultural sensitivity, hydrology and flood context,
  historical interpretation, accessibility, and release?

  documentation structure without creating parallel authority?
- Which current contract and schema families own county composition, layers,
  claims, citations, runtime envelopes, and reason codes?

### Source and evidence

- Which current authoritative sources support each narrowly worded plan claim?
- What rights, attribution, retention, redistribution, and withdrawal conditions
  apply to each source and asset?
- Which sources are authoritative for public military history, and which details
  must be generalized or denied?
- Which sources and qualified reviewers govern corrections, detention, and
  institutional public context?
- Which Nation-authored or community-governed sources and review relationships
  are required for Indigenous history, identity, governance, and sensitive-place

## 13. Maintenance, migration, correction, and rollback

### Review triggers

Re-review this README when any of the following changes materially:

- the sibling build plan or county-index row;
- accepted Directory Rules, Focus Mode ADR status, or final path disposition;
- Focus semantic contracts, schemas, policy, validators, runtime envelopes, or
  finite-outcome vocabulary;
- source admission, rights, terms, cadence, or correction behavior;
- military, corrections, detention, privacy, cultural, infrastructure, river,
  flood, property, emergency, or public-safety review posture;
- county-specific fixtures, runtime implementation, release eligibility, or
  public reliance;
- correction, withdrawal, supersession, or rollback requirements.

### Structural migration rule

Any future move from this tracked compatibility lane must:

1. identify one accepted destination and authority owner;
2. inventory all repository and known external consumers;
3. preserve Git history and stable document identity;
4. repair links, fragments, indexes, templates, generated references, and tests;
5. prevent both old and new paths from becoming writable authorities;
6. validate the full base-to-head migration diff;
7. record deprecation, correction, and rollback behavior;
8. retain or retire the old path only through the accepted migration decision.

Do not create a parallel Leavenworth tree as a shortcut.

### Documentation correction

A factual, safety, privacy, cultural, rights, or authority defect in this README
should be corrected through a scoped pull request that identifies the affected
claim, supporting evidence, prior wording, replacement wording, review need, and
any downstream references. Do not silently rewrite release or source history.

### Rollback

Before merge, abandon or close the feature branch and draft pull request. After
merge, use a transparent revert or forward-fix pull request against the actual
merged commit. A documentation revert cannot withdraw released data, invalidate
caches, or complete a public correction; those remain separate governed actions.
