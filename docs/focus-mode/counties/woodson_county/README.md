## 5. Ecology, wildlife, and geoprivacy boundary

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **General habitat context and exact biological location are different object
> classes.** A public page that names a species, habitat, wildlife area, or
> management practice does not automatically authorize KFM to republish exact
> occurrences, derive hotspots, or reveal withheld patterns.

### Source-role distinctions

| Information class | Valid bounded role | Must not become |
|---|---|---|
| General landscape or habitat description | County-scale ecological context | Exact species-occurrence or refuge-use geometry |
| Public wildlife-area description | Stewardship and management context | Personalized hunting advice or hotspot prediction |
| Observation or survey data | Evidence candidate after rights and sensitivity review | Automatic public point layer |
| Generalized public layer | Reviewed public-safe derivative | Canonical observation store or proof of completeness |
| Map styling or density surface | Interpretive display | Evidence, population estimate, or hidden-location confirmation |
| Generated narrative | Explanation over resolved evidence | Ecological authority or source truth |

### Public-output controls

A future public-safe ecology product requires, at minimum:

1. admitted source identity and role;
2. rights and redistribution review;
3. sensitivity and misuse assessment;
4. documented geometry generalization, suppression, or omission;
5. a transform or representation receipt where precision changes;
6. temporal and completeness limitations;
7. reviewer identity and review state;
8. an `EvidenceRef` resolving to an `EvidenceBundle` for consequential claims;
9. policy and release decisions;
10. correction and withdrawal handling.

### Finite outcomes for ecology requests

| Request class | Default bounded outcome | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Explain the Cross Timbers at generalized scale | `ANSWER` only after evidence/release closure | Stable public-safe context may be supportable |
| Show exact nests, roosts, sensitive occurrences, or refuge-use patterns | `DENY` | Harmful precision and misuse risk |
| Show hunting hotspots or predict where animals will be | `DENY` | Converts stewardship data into targeting assistance |
| Confirm whether a withheld feature exists near a user-supplied point | `DENY` | Inference can defeat geoprivacy |
| Explain why geometry is generalized | `ANSWER` after policy/evidence closure | Trust-visible limitation is appropriate |
| Answer from generated prose without resolved evidence | `ABSTAIN` | Generated language is not evidence |

## 6. Recreation, access, property, and currentness boundary

A location shown on a map does not establish entry, parking, trail, cabin,
campsite, hunting, fishing, shoreline, or adjacent-land rights. A dated public
source does not establish current availability or legality.

### Required distinctions

| Surface | What it may support | What it cannot determine |
|---|---|---|
| Park or recreation page | Source-labeled public-use context | Current availability, universal accessibility, personal permission, or legal compliance |
| Reservation or facility listing | Candidate current-status redirect | Evergreen cabin, campsite, restroom, trail, office, or facility state |
| Permit or regulation page | Current rule source when admitted and time-bounded | Personalized legal advice or permission for a specific place/date |
| Public-land map | General orientation | Private-land exclusion, survey boundary, shoreline right, or access guarantee |
| Boundary plat or survey material | Administrative/survey context | Title, ownership, easement, or trespass conclusion |
| Road or route page | Official current routing when fit | Static road-open, route-safe, or emergency-access claim |
| AI response | Bounded explanation and redirect | Permission, legal determination, or live availability |

### Default request handling

| User request | Outcome | Candidate reason code |
|---|---:|---|
| “Is this trail, campsite, cabin, or facility open now?” | `ABSTAIN` | `CURRENT_RECREATION_STATUS_REQUIRES_AUTHORITY` |
| “Can I enter, camp, hunt, fish, or park here?” | `ABSTAIN` / `DENY` | `PUBLIC_CONTEXT_NOT_ACCESS_PERMISSION` |
| “Is this activity legal here today?” | `ABSTAIN` / `DENY` | `RECREATION_REGULATION_NOT_PERSONALLY_DETERMINED` |
| “Does this line prove my property or shoreline right?” | `DENY` | `SHORELINE_OR_PROPERTY_RIGHT_DETERMINATION_DENIED` |
| “Is this road or route open and safe now?” | `ABSTAIN` | `OFFICIAL_CURRENT_SAFETY_CHANNEL_REQUIRED` |
| “What agency should I check?” | `ANSWER` after current redirect validation | Authority routing is a bounded public use |

Currentness-bearing content needs retrieval time, valid time where available,
source update semantics, an expiry or revalidation rule, stale-state behavior, and
an official redirect. A cached record without those controls must not silently
remain an `ANSWER`.

## 7. Reservoir, water-safety, and infrastructure boundary

> [!WARNING]
> **A lake measurement or project page is not a safety decision.** Water level,
> water quality, closures, weather, water-safety information, shoreline
> management, project planning, and infrastructure condition are separate source
> roles with different time and consequence requirements.

### Anti-collapse matrix

| Source or information role | Bounded future use | Prohibited collapse |
|---|---|---|
| Reservoir/project description | Stable, source-labeled public context | Current operational state or infrastructure-condition claim |
| Lake-level observation | Time-stamped scientific/operational observation | Launch, boating, swimming, crossing, or travel-safety verdict |
| Water-safety guidance | Official current-authority redirect | KFM-generated safety assurance |
| Water-quality or health advisory | Current qualified authority and explicit scope | Generalized “safe water” or “unsafe water” claim beyond source scope |
| Closure or facility-status notice | Expiring official-status carrier | Evergreen availability or access truth |
| Shoreline-management material | Administrative program context | Title, ownership, boundary, or permission determination |
| Survey/boundary plat | Survey or administrative record | Legal title or adjacent-land conclusion |
| Dam/project infrastructure | High-level released public context only | Condition, control-system, vulnerability, tactical, or security detail |
| Weather, road, or emergency source | Official current redirect | Static KFM warning or incident status |

### Required outcomes

| Question | Default outcome | Why |
|---|---:|---|
| “Does this lake level mean the ramp is usable?” | `ABSTAIN` | Observation does not determine site-specific usability or safety |
| “Is it safe to boat or swim now?” | `ABSTAIN` | Requires current competent authority and fit source scope |
| “Is the water safe or drinkable?” | `ABSTAIN` / `DENY` | Water-quality and health determinations require qualified current evidence |
| “Who owns this shoreline?” | `DENY` | KFM is not title, survey, or legal authority |
| “What is the dam's condition or weak point?” | `DENY` | Infrastructure-security and harmful-precision boundary |
| “Is there an emergency or closure?” | `ABSTAIN` plus current official redirect | KFM is not an alerting or emergency authority |
| “Why are project details limited?” | `ANSWER` after evidence/policy closure | Trust-visible safety explanation is appropriate |

A future reservoir layer must carry source role, retrieval time, temporal scope,
limitations, sensitivity, review state, release state, and correction path. It
must not imply that rendered geometry or current map appearance proves safe use.

## 8. Finite outcomes and evidence closure

### Runtime outcome contract

| Outcome | Required condition | Woodson example |
|---|---|---|
| `ANSWER` | Evidence is resolved, source role is fit, rights and sensitivity permit use, time and scope are explicit, policy allows, and released support exists | Generalized explanation of the plan-defined Cross Timbers/Toronto Lake context |
| `ABSTAIN` | Evidence, rights, currentness, authority, release state, or scope is insufficient | Current trail, cabin, campsite, closure, road, lake-level, or water-safety question |
| `DENY` | The request crosses wildlife precision, targeting, property/legal, access, or infrastructure-security boundaries | Exact roost, hunting hotspot, shoreline-right, or dam-vulnerability request |
| `ERROR` | Resolver, schema, policy, citation, catalog, or runtime processing fails | Evidence resolution or policy evaluation cannot complete safely |

### Minimum consequential-claim chain

```text
SourceDescriptor
  -> SourceIntakeRecord
  -> EvidenceRef
  -> EvidenceBundle
  -> PolicyDecision
  -> ReviewRecord
  -> PromotionDecision / ReleaseManifest
  -> governed API response
  -> visible correction and rollback references
```

Skipping a link does not make a claim approximately complete. It narrows the
claim, produces `ABSTAIN` or `DENY`, or blocks the affected release.

### Illustrative no-network fixture families

| Fixture family | Expected result | What it proves |
|---|---:|---|
| Generalized landscape context with resolved synthetic evidence | `ANSWER` | Envelope and citation behavior only |

A passing documentation, schema, fixture, or runtime test would prove only the
named behavior at the tested version. It would not prove source truth, rights,

establish, at a minimum:

- stable identity and version;
- source authority and source role;
- rights and attribution obligations;
- ecological, access, property, safety, and infrastructure sensitivity;

## 10. Smallest safe follow-up

**PROPOSED:** after this documentation-only slice, the smallest useful follow-up
is one no-network, public-safe evidence exercise for a single generalized
landscape or agency-role claim already identified by the sibling plan. It should
not begin with live recreation status, exact wildlife data, a public map, or a
model endpoint.

### Recommended sequence

1. Re-pin current main and repeat overlap/path checks.
2. Select one narrowly worded, non-current, non-sensitive claim from the plan.
3. Identify the exact source role and verify the current authoritative source.
4. Record rights, attribution, scope, retrieval time, and limitations.
5. Decide whether any geometry is needed; prefer county-scale or omitted geometry.
6. Create a resolvable evidence chain using existing accepted object families.
7. Add deterministic synthetic `ANSWER`, `ABSTAIN`, `DENY`, and `ERROR` cases.
8. Prove negative behavior for exact wildlife, access/property, stale status,
   lake-level safety, and infrastructure requests.
9. Obtain ecology, rights, policy, and release review appropriate to the claim.
10. Keep release and publication on HOLD until every required gate is evidenced.

### Follow-up non-goals

- no live connector activation;
- no source-visible coordinate copied automatically into a public layer;
- no exact wildlife or hunting-hotspot fixture;
- no current trail, cabin, campsite, closure, lake, road, or emergency claim;
- no property, title, shoreline, water-safety, or infrastructure determination;
- no browser-to-canonical-store or browser-to-model path;
- no county-specific parallel contract, schema, policy, source, or release
  authority;
- no migration to a second Focus Mode tree without accepted authority.

## 12. Maintenance, correction, and rollback

### Review triggers

Re-review this README when any of the following changes materially:

- sibling build-plan scope or status;
- source authority, URL, terms, rights, cadence, or content;
- ecological sensitivity or wildlife geoprivacy policy;
- recreation, access, property, shoreline, water-safety, road, emergency, or

- Focus Mode contracts, schemas, policy, resolver, runtime, or release machinery;
- correction, withdrawal, cache invalidation, or rollback behavior;
- county-lane placement or ADR status;
- known inbound links, anchors, aliases, mirrors, or generated navigation.

### Documentation maintenance rules

- Preserve this path until a reviewed migration says otherwise.
- Keep one H1, stable section headings, repository-relative links, and a final
  newline.
- Treat the sibling plan as planning lineage; do not silently rewrite its source
  checks or maturity claims through this README.
- Do not copy canonical source, evidence, policy, release, or published objects
  into this directory.
- Keep external facts source-labeled and time-bounded; do not promote a dated plan
  statement into current truth.
- Surface conflicts rather than blending incompatible source roles.
- Update direct references when an authorized rename or migration occurs.
- Record material corrections visibly rather than silently replacing relied-upon
  public claims.

### Rollback and correction

Before merge, rollback is to close or abandon the unmerged PR and branch; remote
branch deletion is a separate action. After merge, use a transparent revert or
forward-fix PR against the actual merged commit. Do not rewrite shared history.

For a future public Woodson release, a Git revert alone may be insufficient.
Correction may also require a `CorrectionNotice`, superseding release manifest,
withdrawal state, cache and tile invalidation, search/index refresh, governed-AI
citation refresh, user-visible notice, and preserved rollback lineage.
