## 5. Groundwater, water-rights, and property boundary

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Water data does not collapse into one authority.** Scientific observations,
> historical geohydrology, administrative boundaries, water-right records,
> agricultural water-use context, and generated interpretation have different
> evidentiary and legal roles.

### Required distinctions

| Question type | Public behavior |
|---|---|
| What is the High Plains/Ogallala aquifer context for Stanton County? | `ANSWER` only from admitted, released, appropriately scoped evidence |
| What did a historical scientific source report? | `ANSWER` with explicit source date and historical scope |
| What is the current level or condition of a particular private well? | `DENY` or `ABSTAIN` unless a separately approved public-safe source and policy explicitly allow it |
| Who owns a water right, what is its priority, or is a use lawful? | `DENY` and route to authoritative current records/qualified interpretation |
| Is a well potable or safe to drink from? | `ABSTAIN` / `DENY`; KFM is not the health/testing authority |
| Does a map prove property ownership, access, or title? | `DENY`; map presence does not establish legal property status |

### Precision posture

Even where a source is publicly accessible, public KFM products should prefer the
minimum precision necessary for the supported county-scale claim. Public availability
alone does not prove fitness for redistribution, aggregation, or public map exposure.

## 6. Agriculture, transportation, and infrastructure boundary

### Agriculture

Agricultural statistics may support county-scale context when the source, year,
aggregation, suppression, and limitations are visible. They must not be used to infer
private operator behavior, individual farm practices, household identity, precise water
use, financial condition, or land/title state.

### Transportation

Public transportation maps and records can support orientation and historical or
administrative context. They must not be transformed into claims about current route
conditions, restricted access, infrastructure weakness, emergency detours, maintenance
operations, or tactical vulnerabilities without a separately governed current product.

### Infrastructure

Infrastructure information is particularly sensitive when precision, function, condition,
or dependency could create misuse risk. The public-safe default is to generalize, omit,
or deny detail that is unnecessary to the county-scale claim.

## 7. Hazard currentness and public-safety boundary

Hazard, flood, weather, fire, road, and emergency information changes quickly. A dated
source can support a historical statement about what was reported at a particular time;
it cannot silently become present-tense operational guidance.

| Requested use | Expected outcome |
|---|---|
| Explain a historical or released flood/hazard context | `ANSWER` with date, source role, and limitations |
| Tell whether a property is safe from flooding | `ABSTAIN` / `DENY` and route to appropriate current authorities |
| Provide live weather/fire/flood/road emergency advice | `ABSTAIN` and redirect to official current channels |
| Identify vulnerable infrastructure or tactical response posture | `DENY` |
| Use one observation as a forecast or warning | `ABSTAIN` because source role is insufficient |

The UI should make stale, missing, restricted, denied, and superseded states visible
rather than hiding them behind a generic map marker or generated answer.

## 8. Finite outcomes and evidence closure

Future Stanton runtime behavior should converge on the shared finite outcomes only after
the relevant shared contracts and policy are verified:

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ANSWER` | Evidence resolves, policy permits, scope/time are fit, and the public artifact is released. |
| `ABSTAIN` | Evidence, currentness, source role, rights, review, or release state is insufficient. |
| `DENY` | Requested output crosses privacy, water-right, property, infrastructure, or safety boundaries. |
| `ERROR` | Resolver, schema, policy, citation, or runtime failure prevents a trusted response. |

### Evidence closure expectation

A consequential Stanton claim should not be presented as authoritative unless its
`EvidenceRef` resolves to an `EvidenceBundle` whose source role, temporal scope, spatial
scope, rights/sensitivity posture, review state, limitations, and release state are fit for
the claim.

Map features, statistics, dashboards, screenshots, tests, generated prose, or model output
cannot substitute for that evidence chain.

merge, use a transparent revert or forward-fix PR against the actual merged commit. Do not
rewrite shared history and do not recreate parallel writable documentation authorities.
