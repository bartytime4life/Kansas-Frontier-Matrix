## 5. Source role and temporal fitness

### Source-role matrix

| Source family | Potential valid role | Must not become |
|---|---|---|
| Kansas Geological Survey regional material | Dated scientific and historical geology/natural-resource context. | Current well, pipeline, facility, production, pressure, leak, emissions, compliance, or health truth. |
| KGS technical project background | Historical project-method and source-family context. | Public operational data entitlement or current asset status. |
| Kansas oil-and-gas regulator | Narrow current regulatory record within documented effective time and terms, after admission. | Property title, environmental-health diagnosis, operator narrative, or emergency guidance. |
| Operator or company material | Attributed first-party statement with explicit limits, if admitted. | Independent regulatory or scientific truth. |
| Monitoring or environmental observations | Specific measured observation with method, time, uncertainty, and scope. | Site-wide cause, exposure, health, legal, or compliance conclusion. |
| Stevens County website and departments | Administrative role and official-routing context. | Title, mineral-right, value, access, zoning, operational status, compliance, or active emergency truth. |
| Appraiser, GIS, tax, parcel, or deeds systems | Restricted or redirect-only administrative source family after rights/privacy review. | Public owner profile, title opinion, mineral ownership, right-of-entry, or person linkage. |
| Emergency services or shelter pages | Official-current routing category. | KFM-issued incident status, evacuation, shelter, fire, leak, or road-safety advice. |
| Generated narrative | Bounded explanation over released evidence. | Evidence, regulator decision, ownership proof, health authority, or release approval. |

### Temporal-fitness classes

| Class | Example | Allowed use | Failure posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Historical/scientific | Dated KGS geology or project material. | Time-attributed regional context. | `ABSTAIN` from current asset, environment, or compliance claims. |
| Administrative routing | County department or public-system link. | Explain responsible office or route. | Do not infer the underlying record or current condition. |
| Regulatory current record | Future admitted regulator record with effective time. | Narrow status exactly within source semantics. | Suppress or `ABSTAIN` when stale, unclear, superseded, or incomplete. |
| Monitoring observation | Future admitted measured value. | Describe the observation and uncertainty. | No causal, health, compliance, or site-wide inference without fit authority. |
| Emergency current | Future official alert or incident route. | Redirect users to official current authority. | Do not cache as durable KFM truth. |
| Generated | AI or templated synthesis. | Explain released evidence and limitations. | `ERROR` or `ABSTAIN` when evidence closure fails. |

### Minimum time fields for future claims

A future claim-bearing card should expose, as applicable:

- source publication, update, or effective date;
- retrieval time and immutable source identity;
- observation start/end time;
- revision, rescission, supersession, or enforcement state;
- expected freshness and expiry;
- last successful verification;
- stale, unavailable, conflicted, or withdrawn state;
- the difference between source time and KFM release time.

Missing time semantics must narrow the claim, suppress the card, or produce
`ABSTAIN`; they must not be hidden behind polished prose.

## 6. Regional energy geology and historical context

The plan's safest first lane is generalized, dated interpretation rather than
asset-level mapping.

### Allowed educational posture

A future released card may explain that checked KGS material describes the
Hugoton natural-gas area as a major southwest Kansas resource and places Stevens
County within a broader geological and development history, provided that:

- every claim resolves to an admitted and released `EvidenceBundle`;
- source dates and source roles are visible;
- spatial presentation remains at the minimum useful county or regional scale;
- historical scientific context is not described as current operations;
- diagrams, excerpts, maps, and derivatives have verified rights;
- limitations state that no ownership, condition, production, leak, health,
  compliance, or emergency conclusion follows;
- corrections and rollback can withdraw unsafe or misleading output.

### Prohibited semantic upgrades

The following transformations are not supported:

| Input | Unsupported upgrade |
|---|---|
| Historical regional geology | “This specific well is active, safe, productive, or leaking.” |
| Technical project description | “KFM may expose well, pressure, log, core, production, or reservoir-model detail.” |
| County administrative route | “This parcel belongs to this person or includes these mineral rights.” |
| General natural-gas history | “This company or site is compliant, responsible, clean, unsafe, or harmful.” |
| A monitoring value | “This measurement proves contamination, exposure, health impact, cause, or legal liability.” |
| A public map or database | “All fields and geometry are public-safe to redistribute or combine.” |
| Model-generated summary | “The model has filled a missing regulatory, property, or health fact.” |

### Scale and aggregation rules

Public-safe context should prefer:

- county or regional narrative;
- generalized non-operational geology;
- historical periodization;
- source-role comparison;
- aggregate, non-person-linked statistics where rights and fitness are closed;
- visible withholding notices rather than hidden asset confirmation.

It should not expose exact asset geometry, tactical infrastructure relationships,
facility condition, vulnerable dependencies, operational telemetry, private
records, or a combination of public layers that reconstructs those details.

## 7. Operational infrastructure and reconstruction risk

Energy infrastructure is policy-significant even when some elements are
individually public.

### Denied-by-default detail

The normal public lane must not provide:

- exact or current well, pipeline, compressor, plant, storage, gathering,
  pressure, production, condition, maintenance, outage, leak, or vulnerability
  detail;
- tactical connectivity or dependency graphs;
- current operational status inferred from stale or indirect sources;
- security-sensitive access, inspection, control, or emergency information;
- facility targeting or route optimization;
- field-level data whose combination reconstructs withheld assets;
- confirmation that a denied feature exists at a queried location.

### Cross-layer reconstruction

A safe transform must evaluate not only each layer but also combinations such
as:

- generalized energy features plus parcel or owner data;
- historical well records plus recent imagery or monitoring;
- pipeline context plus road, facility, utility, or emergency layers;
- public facility names plus operational metadata;
- county GIS plus tax, deeds, mineral, lease, or living-person information;
- model inference over withheld or partial geometry.

Where combinations can materially reconstruct sensitive detail, KFM should
generalize further, omit fields, restrict zoom/export/query, deny the request,
or withhold the layer entirely.

### Visible public notice

A future Energy Operations Boundary panel should explain:

- why regional geology can be shown while asset detail is withheld;
- that withholding is not confirmation of a hidden asset;
- that KFM is not an operational or emergency system;
- that current status requires competent official authority;
- how to report a correction or harmful exposure;
- that public availability elsewhere does not automatically establish KFM
  rights, fitness, or safe amplification.

## 8. Property, mineral rights, and living-person privacy

County routing surfaces create a strong temptation to infer property or mineral
facts. This lane rejects that upgrade.

### Non-determination boundary

KFM must not determine or imply:

- surface or mineral ownership;
- title quality or chain of title;
- lease, royalty, unit, pooling, easement, lien, or severance status;
- value, tax liability, appraisal accuracy, or legal description sufficiency;
- right of entry, access, trespass, drilling authority, or permit entitlement;
- zoning or land-use compliance;
- a living person's residence, contact, holdings, income, or business
  relationship;
- a causal connection between an owner, farm, operator, property, and an
  environmental condition.

### Source-role constraints

| Source family | Safe role | Unsafe use |
|---|---|---|
| County GIS or parcel viewer | Official-route explanation or generalized geography after review. | Title, access, ownership, mineral, or legal conclusion. |
| Appraiser or tax system | Administrative role and redirect. | Living-person profile, valuation advice, or ownership proof. |
| Register of Deeds | Explain where official records are maintained. | KFM title opinion or machine-derived chain of title. |
| Regulatory well record | Narrow regulator-defined record. | Mineral owner, leaseholder, right-of-entry, or private-person inference. |
| Operator filing | Attributed filing within its role. | Independent title or compliance proof. |
| AI synthesis | Explain why KFM abstains or denies. | Fill missing owner, mineral, lease, or legal facts. |

### Public response posture

A user asking “Who owns the minerals under this parcel?” or “Can this company
enter this land?” should receive `DENY` or a narrowly scoped official-record
routing explanation—not a synthesized ownership or legal answer.

<a id="9-groundwater-environmental-health-regulation-and-emergency-currentness"></a>

## 9. Groundwater, environmental health, regulation, and emergency currentness

These are high-stakes, time-sensitive, and source-role-sensitive questions.

### Groundwater and environmental health

General geology or energy history cannot establish:

- contamination presence or absence;
- drinking-water safety or potability;
- methane migration or exposure;
- health risk, diagnosis, causation, or remediation need;
- private-well condition, yield, or safety;
- site-specific emissions, leak probability, or environmental impact;
- responsibility or liability for an observed condition.

A future narrow answer requires fit current authority, method, sample location
and time, uncertainty, review, privacy and rights controls, and a claim scope
that does not exceed the source.

### Regulatory and compliance claims

A future regulator record may support only what the regulator actually issued,
for the stated asset, party, time, status, and jurisdiction. KFM must not:

- infer compliance from absence of an enforcement record;
- infer noncompliance from a complaint, permit, inspection, or stale entry;
- merge operator, regulator, scientific, county, and monitoring records into an
  unlabeled verdict;
- represent a proposed, pending, appealed, superseded, or expired action as
  final;
- provide legal advice or predict enforcement;
- use a model to classify a company or property as compliant or noncompliant.

### Emergency and safety routing

Questions about a current leak, fire, explosion, evacuation, shelter, road
closure, or other emergency require official current channels. Static county
pages may establish that an office or route exists; they do not establish an
active incident or protective action.

KFM should `ABSTAIN` and provide verified category-level routing without copying
an old alert into durable truth. It must not delay emergency response by
presenting itself as the authoritative current channel.

## 10. Finite outcomes and candidate reason codes

## 17. Maintenance, migration, correction, and rollback

### Maintenance triggers

Review this README when:

- the Stevens plan changes;
- the Sherman foreign-plan defect is corrected or its history is clarified;
- the county index is fully recomputed;
- ADR-0027 changes status or another accepted decision settles Focus placement;
- shared Focus contracts, schemas, policy, validators, or receipt requirements
  change;
- a source is admitted, superseded, withdrawn, or changes rights;
- critical-infrastructure, property/privacy, environmental-health, groundwater,
  regulatory, or emergency posture changes;
- a fixture, API/UI slice, release candidate, correction, or rollback object is
  added;
- a harmful inference or public misunderstanding is reported.

### Structural migration

Any future move or rename must:

1. pin the accepted authority and current source/target bytes;
2. resolve the Stevens/Sherman identity defect without treating the foreign copy
   as a second valid authority;
3. inventory inbound references, generated consumers, branches, external
   consumers, and fragment links;
4. preserve Git history and stable identity where possible;
5. prevent simultaneous writable old and new homes;
6. update indexes, registries, validation, links, and receipts atomically within
   a governed migration sequence;
7. provide compatibility behavior and exit criteria where required;
8. validate rollback does not recreate parallel authority.

This README does not authorize such migration.

### Documentation correction

For an error before merge, update the branch and regenerate the receipt if
meaning-bearing README bytes change. After merge, use a transparent correction
or forward-fix PR with the prior commit and affected claim identified.

### Future public correction

A public Stevens product would require more than a Git revert. Depending on the
error, correction may need to:

- withdraw or supersede a card, layer, payload, tile, export, or narrative;
- invalidate caches, search indexes, graphs, vector indexes, and AI context;
- remove harmful exact detail while retaining audit evidence;
- notify affected stewards and downstream consumers;
- preserve correction reason, prior release identity, replacement identity, and
  effective time;
- handle rights, privacy, infrastructure, health, regulatory, or emergency
  consequences;
- verify public aliases no longer resolve to withdrawn material.

### Rollback

Before merge, rollback is to close or abandon the draft PR and branch. Remote
branch deletion requires separate authority.

After merge, use a transparent revert or bounded forward fix against the actual
merged commits. Do not rewrite shared history. Revert the README and generated
receipt coherently. Do not use rollback to restore the misleading Stevens/
Sherman exact-tree duplication as a supposedly canonical state without
explicitly acknowledging that tradeoff.
