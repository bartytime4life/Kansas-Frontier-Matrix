## 4. Proposed county proof slice

The build plan proposes a county proof slice centered on four connected but non-interchangeable concerns:

1. **Generalized landscape orientation** — Decatur County, Oberlin, and Sappa Creek context.
2. **Cultural-authority discipline** — Northern Cheyenne history must not be represented through settler or local museum sources alone.
3. **Sensitive-place restraint** — route, campsite, burial, sacred, artifact, archaeological, descendant, and living-person precision remains withheld by default.
4. **Operational currentness** — dated drought and weather material remains attributable, expiring official context rather than durable KFM safety or legal truth.

| Proposed question | Bounded planning answer | Required hold |
|---|---|---|
| What landscape story could the county lane explain? | Generalized Sappa Creek and Oberlin orientation with visible evidence and limitations. | No source activation or environmental claim until evidence is reverified. |
| How could Northern Cheyenne history be approached? | By foregrounding Nation-authoritative evidence, review state, disagreement, and source role. | No public cultural narrative from local public-memory sources alone. |
| What can be mapped about the 1878 Exodus? | At most reviewed, generalized, public-safe context. | No exact route, campsite, burial, sacred, artifact, or archaeological location. |
| What role can a museum play? | A bounded local public-memory and collection source whose provenance, terminology, rights, and limitations are visible. | It cannot become complete cultural authority or substitute for Nation review. |
| What can be said about drought or hazards? | The role, date, scope, and destination of official-current sources. | No standing legal, water-use, agricultural, road, fire, travel, health, or emergency conclusion. |
| What could the map carry? | Released, generalized, public-safe contextual layers only. | No direct internal-store, restricted cultural, sensitive-location, or current operational-detail path. |

This thesis is **PROPOSED**. It is not evidence that the named cards, layers, panels, payloads, routes, fixtures, policies, or release objects exist.

## 5. Source-role anti-collapse

The linked plan records source checks dated 2026-06-08. Those checks were not repeated in this README update. Each source family must be reverified and admitted for its actual role before use.

| Source family named by the plan | Candidate role | Must not become |
|---|---|---|
| Northern Cheyenne Nation-authoritative material | Cultural authority, terminology, scope, sensitivity, and review direction. | A symbolic citation added after a settler-authored narrative is already fixed. |
| Local museum or public-memory material | Local institutional history, collection context, and provenance leads. | Complete Northern Cheyenne history, consent, site authority, or unrestricted reuse permission. |
| Kansas historical records | Documentary and state public-history context. | Nation authority, cultural consent, or sole adjudicator of contested accounts. |
| Archaeological, burial, or sacred-place material | Restricted review input when appropriate and authorized. | Public coordinate, route, reconstruction, collection-location, or access data. |
| City or county material | Civic orientation and dated official notices. | Durable drought, legal, water-use, agricultural, road, or emergency truth. |
| NWS and other competent operational authorities | Official-current weather, hazard, drought, and safety routing. | A license for KFM or AI to rewrite, delay, simplify, or independently interpret active guidance. |
| Broad hydrographic or landscape sources | Generalized Sappa Creek and landscape context. | Flood, access, ownership, current condition, or safety authority. |
| Generated language | Bounded explanation over resolved evidence. | Evidence, cultural authority, policy, approval, current operational status, or publication authority. |

### Minimum source-admission questions

Before any source participates in a public claim, verify:

- stable source identity, issuing authority, and actual source role;
- cultural authority, consent, representation, and review obligations;
- source fitness for the exact historical, landscape, drought, or safety claim;
- publication, retrieval, observation, valid, effective, and expiry times;
- draft, final, superseded, withdrawn, corrected, or contested state;

<a id="6-cultural-authority-and-historical-narrative"></a>

## 6. Cultural authority and historical narrative

### Representation rule

The linked plan identifies Northern Cheyenne Exodus memory as a central proposed theme. That theme cannot be advanced by treating local labels, memorial language, settler accounts, museum presentation, or generated synthesis as neutral or complete authority.

A future candidate must show, at minimum:

1. which Northern Cheyenne authority or reviewer is empowered for the proposed scope;
2. which terms, claims, narrative frames, images, and map generalizations were reviewed;
3. which accounts disagree and how the disagreement remains visible;
4. which details are withheld entirely rather than merely hidden by default;
5. what rights and reuse limits apply to text, images, artifacts, oral histories, and collection records;
6. how corrections, objections, withdrawal, and supersession propagate.

### Precision rule

The following content defaults to **DENY**, exclusion, generalization, or restricted review:

- burial, sacred, ceremonial, or memorial-sensitive places;
- exact Exodus route, campsite, incident, artifact, collection, or archaeological geometry;
- descendant, family, or living-person linkage;
- reconstructable combinations of narrative, imagery, distances, landmarks, and coordinates;
- access implications for private, restricted, stewarded, or culturally sensitive places;
- unpublished or restricted collection details.

### Contested-claim rule

If source roles, chronology, terminology, causation, or interpretation conflict, KFM must preserve the dispute and narrow the answer. AI must not reconcile contested history into a single fluent conclusion. An unresolved conflict produces `ABSTAIN`, `DENY`, or `ERROR` according to the request and evidence state.

## 7. Time, currentness, and official authority

The Decatur proof slice is time-sensitive in several distinct ways:

| Time or state | Meaning | Failure posture |
|---|---|---|
| Historical-event time | When a documented event or account concerns. | Do not collapse later interpretation into contemporaneous evidence. |
| Source publication time | When a page, notice, record, or dataset was issued. | Do not substitute retrieval time. |
| Retrieval/check time | When KFM or the build-plan author last checked a source. | Mark stale or abstain after its fitness window. |
| Observation time | When a drought, weather, or environmental condition was observed. | Do not present it as present condition without current authority. |
| Valid/effective time | When a proclamation, notice, warning, or decision applies. | A dated notice cannot silently acquire indefinite effect. |
| Expiry time | When operational information stops being safely reusable. | Suppress, mark stale, or redirect if absent or expired. |
| Cultural-review time | When an authorized reviewer assessed terminology, scope, sensitivity, and representation. | No implied approval from document date. |
| Release time | When a governed release decision became effective. | A commit or merge is not release time. |
| Correction time | When a claim was corrected, superseded, withdrawn, or rolled back. | Preserve lineage and prevent stale reappearance. |

### Drought and hazard rule

A dated city, county, state, or federal drought notice may support a narrowly attributed statement about that notice at its valid time. It must not support an unqualified claim that:

- a drought emergency remains active now;
- a specific water-use restriction, agricultural impact, insurance consequence, road condition, or legal duty applies;
- current fire-weather, health, travel, river, or emergency conditions follow from the old notice;
- generated language may simplify or extend the issuing authority's guidance.

### Official-current routing rule

Active warnings, road conditions, evacuation, shelter, fire-weather, water-use, emergency, and similar operational requests require a competent official-current source, explicit valid time, and bounded expiry. Without that closure, the runtime must **ABSTAIN** and route the user to the responsible authority.

## 8. Public-safe and sensitive boundaries

| Requested or implied content | Default outcome | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|
| Generalized Decatur County, Oberlin, or Sappa Creek orientation | `ANSWER` only after evidence and release closure | Public-safe contextual interpretation may be possible. |
| Explanation of why Nation-authoritative review is required | `ANSWER` from governing documentation | The boundary itself can be explained without supplying the withheld narrative. |
| Northern Cheyenne cultural narrative without appropriate review | `ABSTAIN` | Cultural authority and representation are unresolved. |
| Local museum or settler framing presented as complete | `ABSTAIN` | Source-role imbalance cannot become sovereign truth. |
| Exact route, campsite, burial, sacred, artifact, archaeology, or sensitive memorial detail | `DENY` | Harmful or culturally inappropriate precision fails closed. |
| Descendant, family, or living-person linkage | `DENY` | Privacy, consent, and cultural sensitivity are unresolved. |
| Current drought, water-use, agricultural, legal, fire, road, health, travel, or emergency conclusion | `ABSTAIN` | Requires competent official-current authority and expiry. |
| Museum asset reuse with unclear rights | `ABSTAIN` or quarantine | Discovery does not grant redistribution or derivative-use rights. |
| AI reconciliation of disputed accounts | `ABSTAIN` or `ERROR` | Generated language cannot adjudicate historical authority. |
| Public client access to RAW, WORK, QUARANTINE, candidate, or internal stores | `ERROR` | Violates the trust membrane. |

### Public map and AI boundary

A future public map or Focus response may consume only released, public-safe artifacts through governed interfaces. It must not:

- expose internal lifecycle paths or source payloads;
- retrieve restricted cultural or museum material directly in the browser;
- use feature visibility as a substitute for policy permission;
- infer withheld locations from neighboring features, narrative, or timing;
- send ungoverned map context directly to a model;
- present a model answer without resolved evidence, policy result, citations, finite outcome, and receipt.
