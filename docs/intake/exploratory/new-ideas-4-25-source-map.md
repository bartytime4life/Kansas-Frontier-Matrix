## Unsafe direct transfers

| Packet pattern | Why direct transfer is unsafe | Required correction |
|---|---|---|
| “Ready-to-commit” loaders, policies, validators, and directory trees | The repository already has stronger or conflicting owners and object families. | Re-derive from current contracts and Directory Rules; implement one dependency-closed gap. |
| Rounded coordinates, normalized names, dates, or taxon strings as an automatic dedupe key | Nearby or similarly named records can represent different entities; one entity can also change names, geometry, or precision. | Emit a match proposal with evidence and confidence; require a reversible decision and preserve every source assertion. |
| Winner-takes-all canonical record | Losing source values, conflicts, and correction paths disappear. | Preserve source-native assertions and derive a selected view with explicit decision lineage. |
| Hard-coded taxonomy authority ranking | Authority depends on taxonomic group, use, date, source scope, and accepted governance. | Preserve source-native concepts and use a versioned, reviewed reconciliation profile. |
| Scientific-name string treated as a stable taxon identity | Homonyms, synonyms, authorship, splits, lumps, and changing circumscriptions break string identity. | Separate NameUsage from TaxonConcept and record typed relations with time and source evidence. |
| GNIS name treated as proof of existence, geometry, boundary, ownership, or legal status | GNIS is a naming source, not universal authority for every downstream claim. | Keep naming role explicit and require separate evidence for feature, geometry, legal, or ownership claims. |
| PLSS described as a universal “ground truth lattice” or “spatial checksum” | Survey records have generations, adjustments, monument loss, georeferencing uncertainty, and jurisdictional/legal limits. | Treat them as survey-control evidence and disclose derivation method, residuals, status, and limitations. |
| Fixed policy-coverage or mutation-score threshold treated as doctrine | Numeric adequacy varies by policy significance, mutant set, equivalent mutants, and missing semantic classes. | Report gap classes and surviving mutants; ratify any thresholds through policy/test ownership. |
| Unpinned network installs in validation workflows | Tool drift or outage can make proof irreproducible or silently alter behavior. | Pin reviewed dependencies and run deterministic no-network fixtures for release-critical checks. |
| Signed AI output treated as evidence or promotion approval | A signature proves origin and integrity under a profile, not factual support or release authority. | Keep AI output subordinate to evidence, policy, independent review, release, and correction. |
| Random jitter used for sensitive locations | Random displacement can leak through repetition, cross-source joins, or map context and is hard to audit. | Use accepted deterministic generalization, withholding, aggregation, or denial policy. |
| Real taxa, coordinates, people, DNA, place notes, keys, or tokens in fixtures | Examples can expose sensitive facts or become joinable. | Use synthetic, non-joinable fixtures and secret references only. |

## Dependency-ordered continuation

### Wave 1 - meaning and authority decisions

1. Define reversible entity reconciliation, match evidence, merge/split decisions, and source-assertion preservation.
2. Define NameUsage, TaxonConcept, typed concept relations, accepted-use context, and temporal lineage.
3. Define place-name authority roles, temporal aliases, feature binding, dispute, and supersession without role collapse.
4. Define survey-control observations, control status, adjustment, georeference, residuals, derived boundaries, and legal-use limits.
5. Define assurance evidence for validators and policies, including mutant class, expected semantic effect, surviving mutant, and waived/equivalent rationale.

### Wave 2 - bounded offline proofs

After the applicable Wave 1 decision closes, admit at most one dependency-closed proof:

- synthetic entity pairs covering exact match, possible match, collision, merge, split, reversal, and retained conflict;
- synthetic taxonomy covering synonym, homonym, split, lump, misapplied name, and unresolved concept;
- synthetic place-name history covering official, variant, historical, disputed, translated, superseded, and unbound names;
- synthetic survey-control observations covering monument status, adjustment, georeference residual, conflicting evidence, and derived-boundary qualification; or
- a no-network validator mutation harness with semantic mutants for fail-open outcome, missing required field, altered authority, stale version, and bypassed denial.

### Wave 3 - source and lane adoption

Live biodiversity, taxonomy, GNIS, BLM, cadastral, policy-engine, or AI integrations remain later work. Each requires:

- current official-source verification and admitted source descriptors;
- accepted identity, authority, rights, sensitivity, and temporal semantics;
- contracts, schemas, policies, fixtures, validators, and correction paths;
- explicit source roles that prevent name, geometry, legal, and ownership authority from collapsing;
- review, release, rollback, and withdrawal behavior; and
- separate exact authorization.

### Recommended next bounded action

**PROPOSED:** start with a cross-domain taxonomy concept decision packet and synthetic fixtures for synonym, homonym, split, lump, misapplied name, unresolved match, and reversal. This closes a real flora/fauna seam without fetching live records, selecting a permanent authority rank, or publishing a distribution claim.
