## Retained non-duplicative ideas

### 1. Preserve authority and maturity as separate axes

A dated assessment should never compress authority and capability into one status word.

| Axis | Example vocabulary | Question answered | Must not imply |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authority / epistemic posture | `CONFIRMED`, `PROPOSED`, `UNKNOWN`, `NEEDS VERIFICATION`, `HOLD`, `DENY`, `ABSTAIN` | How strongly may the claim or decision be relied on for the stated scope? | Code execution, deployment, source activation, release, or publication |
| Capability maturity | `IMPLEMENTED`, `PARTIAL`, `DRAFT`, `DEPRECATED`, `ABSENT`, `NOT INSPECTED` | What implementation evidence exists for the stated capability at the pinned snapshot? | Acceptance, evidence sufficiency, rights clearance, policy approval, review, or public fitness |

Examples:

- an accepted directory decision can have partial implementation;
- an implemented validator can enforce a proposed inactive profile;
- a confirmed source fact can remain held from activation because rights or sensitivity are unresolved; and
- a complete fixture packet can remain unproved in target runtime, release, or public-client conditions.

**PROPOSED intake rule:** future dated source maps should record both axes whenever they make a material repository-maturity claim. If either axis was not inspected, say so rather than selecting the nearest optimistic label.

### 2. Use a portfolio view to sequence source reviews without activating sources

The report's ten dossiers become useful when treated as a review portfolio rather than a bulk source registry. A portfolio can expose why one bounded source review is ready for consideration while another must remain held, but it cannot replace any source-specific record or decision.

The portfolio should preserve:

- stable candidate and domain identifiers;
- source role and allowed/prohibited claim types;
- current `SourceDescriptor` and rights/currentness assessment references, when they exist;
- currentness trigger and next verification date;
- rights, redistribution, automation, caching, attribution, retention, and correction questions;
- sensitivity, sovereignty, privacy, safety, and public-precision posture;
- required domain, source, legal, privacy, security, accessibility, sovereign, and independent reviewers;
- exact no-network acceptance fixture and negative cases;
- dependency, blocker, and deferral reasons;
- correction, withdrawal, and rollback consequences; and
- an explicit statement that ordering does not create admission, activation, promotion, release, or publication authority.

## Proposed source-activation portfolio view

**PROPOSED, non-authoritative sequence:**

1. **Fail-closed boundary first.** Record denied and held classes before ranking attractive sources. Genomic person-level data, reveal keys, exact sensitive cultural/ecological locations, and life-safety or tactical inferences stay outside ordinary public activation unless a separately authorized controlled purpose exists.
2. **Fixture-first candidates.** Prefer one public-safe, version-pinned, no-network source fixture whose identity, role, rights, time, units, quality, evidence, correction, and rollback can be tested without a live endpoint.
3. **Conditional agency integrations.** Defer sources with unresolved terms, stable identifiers, correction semantics, automation permission, staleness, precision, or field-level sensitivity until the named authority closes them.
4. **High-risk domains separately.** Cultural, Tribal, living-person, private-well, exact ecology, community-survey, infrastructure, and comparable sources require their own qualified authority and cannot inherit approval from a general portfolio row.
5. **No wave promotion.** Completing one portfolio wave changes review readiness only. Every source still follows its own governed admission, evidence, policy, review, promotion, correction, rollback, and release path.

This sequence adapts the report's cross-domain comparison while rejecting its dated product/version details as current evidence.
