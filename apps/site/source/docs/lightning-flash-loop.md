# Lightning layers and observed flash loop

The NASA LIS/OTD layer is a fixed 1995–2014, approximately 0.5° flash-rate climatology. Its WMTS date is a tile carrier, not an event date. It must not be replayed as individual lightning strikes. The NOAA nowCOAST layer is a separate 15-minute, 8 × 8 km strike-density raster. Neither layer supplies individual flash coordinates.

The **Observed satellite flash loop** is a third, opt-in layer. The Site requests recent NOAA GOES-19 GLM flash-centroid GeoJSON through the independent, public [Atmostorm API](https://atmostorm.com/api-docs/endpoints). GLM is a total-lightning optical sensor: it includes in-cloud flashes, and each centroid is a cloud-top estimate rather than a ground-contact strike. The displayed marker radius and violet halo encode playback time and selected persistence, not measured physical flash extent, intensity, or location uncertainty. Energy and area values remain in the response for future inspected details; marker size does not currently encode them. [NOAA product metadata](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/metadata/landing-page/bin/iso?id=gov.noaa.ncdc%3AC01527) describes the flash time and energy-weighted centroid.

The loop offers 5, 15, 30, 60 and 180 minute request windows; speed, pulse persistence, opacity, scrubbing, step and repeat controls are independent of the climatology and density opacity controls. All playback uses returned flash times and coordinates; it does not interpolate missing events. Reduced-motion mode suppresses autoplay and animated halos. The upstream `state=KS` shortcut is treated as a Kansas-area query, not a verified state-boundary intersection; near-edge points are disclosed. A quiet or unavailable feed is not an all-clear.

The Site route fixes the upstream host and allowed request parameters, caps responses at 4 MB and 5,000 features, enforces a 12-second deadline, rejects redirects and changed source contracts, and marks a cap as partial. A response older than five minutes is withheld. The browser refreshes every minute while open and clears displayed points on a failed refresh. Source availability and map rendering remain separate states. This is external observational context, not KFM evidence or an emergency warning service.

The Site application owns these `app/` modules and its same-origin API; this avoids a second catalog or evidence authority. The checked Directory Rules v2 guidance places Site behavior in the existing application and behavior-linked documentation in `docs/`. To roll back this feature, restore the prior Site version or remove the `app/api/lightning/flashes` route and `LightningFlashLoop` mounting; the NASA and NOAA density layers remain independent. Recheck the upstream API contract and the Site owner-private access before any deployment.

## Initial timed exploration (Site v141, historical checkpoint)

The climate card now switches to observed flashes and hides the climate and density rasters. The studio defaults to the latest three hours with disjoint 15-minute slices. Half-hour and hourly slices partition the same returned observations, including empty intervals and a final inclusive end boundary. The activity bars count returned centroids; they are not an intensity or completeness estimate. Individual pulses retain their shorter persistence controls. Scrubbing, previous/next, speed and loop work with either view.

The connected Atmostorm endpoint documents a maximum 180-minute window. At this checkpoint daily playback was visibly unavailable; no daily events or historical strike times are inferred from the 1995–2014 average. The provider cap and stale/error behavior still apply. The following update connects a separately verified timestamped archive for selected historical intervals.

## Historical calendar and HMS visibility (October 4, 2026 UTC)

The lightning studio now offers Recent flashes and Year / month archive. The archive has explicit year, month, day, UTC hour, interval (15/30/60 minutes), and start-minute controls. It checks NOAA GOES-East GLM-L2-LCFA files from July 5, 2017 onward; a selectable date is not a coverage claim. GOES-16 is selected before April 4, 2025 at 15:00 UTC, GOES-19 from that operational transition onward. The source is NOAA's public NODD archive, documented at https://registry.opendata.aws/noaa-goes/.

Only the selected interval is retrieved. Its manifest pins object identities and sizes. Requests permit fixed NOAA hosts and product paths, at most two hourly listings of 200 entries, 128 KiB listing bodies, one 4 MiB object per request, and a 25-second server deadline. The browser reads two files concurrently, decodes them using pinned h5wasm 0.10.3 loaded from same-origin static assets on demand, releases each in-memory HDF5 file, and caps the view at 50,000 Kansas-area centroids. No raw archive is persisted or bulk-month download started. All selected files must decode before the map changes. Selection changes and cancellation withhold old results. Source-supplied dense/compact attributes, file identity, quality flags and the legacy signed-millisecond versus modern unsigned-second clock are checked; unsupported formats remain errors.

The next 20-second file is included because its completed flashes may start before the selected interval ends. Flashes are filtered to the half-open requested interval, quality flag zero, and the disclosed Kansas-area bounding box, then deduplicated. Missing file slots produce PARTIAL coverage, never fabricated events. Archive age is distinct from recent-feed freshness. GLM still gives total-lightning cloud-top centroids, not ground strike locations or complete strike counts. The 1995–2014 NASA climate average remains separate.

Validation used a full real May 21, 2024, 21:00–21:15 UTC interval: 46/46 files, 1,062 accepted Kansas-area centroids. Small original files from 2018, 2024 and 2026 provide offline format regressions; the 2024 count and packed times were cross-checked with Python h5py. This does not prove continuous historical coverage or rendered-browser acceptance.

NOAA HMS October 3, 2026 KML was retrievable with no Kansas-area footprints; October 2 contained two. The smoke card now shows loaded/empty status explicitly and can search at most seven recent publications for a nonempty day. It labels the result with its historical date and any unchecked newer days. Missing publications remain distinct from valid empty days. Default seven-day playback starts at the beginning of its requested range.

## Continuous year playback (October 4, 2026 UTC)

Year / month archive now defaults to **Loop a year**. Choose only a year and press Play year; the studio automatically loads and displays complete UTC hours in chronological order. Each still frame contains that hour's returned centroids. Quiet, checked hours still advance. The last completed year is the default; 2017 starts July 5 and the current year ends at its last completed UTC hour. Leap days and the GOES-East transition use the same archive calendar and source guards. The repeat toggle wraps at the selected year boundary; otherwise playback stops there.

Month buttons and a day slider allow optional calendar jumps without specifying an interval. Pause, restart, previous/next day and load/retry controls are available. Changing year stops playback; seeking a day preserves playing/paused state and checks the newly selected hour. Reduced motion disables autoplay, while calendar navigation loads still frames. Hiding the page pauses and cancels pending work. Generation guards and cancellation prevent a superseded selection from reaching the map.

This is complete-hour streaming, not a fast annual summary or representative sampling. The UI explains that each hour can take a minute or longer to retrieve and a full year can take days. Only the current decoded snapshot is retained, and the same bounded two-file loader and 50,000-centroid limit apply. No bulk annual download, new persistence, background job, or new provider is introduced. Failed hours stop playback and remain unknown; partial hours disclose missing files. The Specific interval mode retains all prior detailed calendar and pulse controls.
