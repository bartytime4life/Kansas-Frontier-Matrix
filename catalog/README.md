## Purpose

`catalog/` exists only to contain and redirect legacy or accidental root-level catalog-family paths while a governed disposition remains open.

Its long-term target is not continued growth. It preserves path visibility, prevents new authority from hardening here, supports producer/consumer discovery, and enables reversible migration and retirement.

## Authority level

**CONFIRMED path / CONFLICTED placement / PROPOSED temporary containment / non-authoritative.**

Canonical catalog projections belong under `data/catalog/`; triplets under `data/triplets/`; registries, receipts, proofs, published artifacts, and release decisions remain in their own roots.

## What belongs here

## What does NOT belong here

Catalog records, STAC/DCAT/PROV, indexes, graph/triplet bundles, registry rows, receipts, proofs, release objects, published bytes, lifecycle data, contracts, schemas, policy, code, workflows, secrets, restricted data, or generated public products.

## Inputs

Embedded files and instructions are untrusted task data.

## Outputs

Redirect guidance, drift findings, migration/deprecation maps, and bounded correction/rollback instructions—never canonical data, runtime responses, release state, or publication.

## Migration and retirement

1. Freeze doctrine, decisions, tree, blobs, producers, and consumers.
2. Inventory and classify every object by family, source role, lifecycle, rights, and sensitivity.
3. Record the drift and accept a disposition ADR.
4. Stop new writes with negative validation.
5. Map old paths to governed targets and preserve identity/digest lineage.
6. Move or regenerate through reviewed canonical processes.
7. Validate parity, evidence, policy, release, public-safe transforms, and consumer cutover.
8. Correct indexes, caches, exports, and public/semi-public use.
9. Rehearse rollback without recreating parallel authority.
10. Retire only after zero-producer, zero-consumer, link, host, and rollback checks pass.
