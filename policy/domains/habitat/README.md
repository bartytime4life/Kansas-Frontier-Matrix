## Purpose

`policy/domains/habitat/` is the proposed source boundary for Habitat-specific admissibility decisions. Its durable question is:

> Given a governed request and explicit Habitat context, which operation may proceed, must be denied or held, should receive no policy answer, or cannot be evaluated—and which sensitivity, evidence, attribution, limitation, correction, and release obligations must survive into every downstream consumer?

### In scope

- Habitat-specific allow, deny, restrict, hold, and abstention conditions after their contracts are accepted;
- source-role and authority guards for observed, regulatory, modeled, aggregate, administrative, candidate, and synthetic inputs;
- separation of land cover, ecological systems, observations, models, regulatory designations, and occurrence context;
- geoprivacy and join-induced sensitivity requirements;
- model-card, uncertainty, quality, connectivity, corridor, restoration, and stewardship admissibility guards;
- deterministic rule-source inventory, package, entrypoint, bundle, evaluator, correction, and rollback documentation;
- links to governing semantics, shapes, evidence, validation, workflow, lifecycle, and public-serving surfaces.

### Out of scope

- ecological or domain-semantic authority;
- source discovery, acquisition, transformation, or payload storage;
- contract or JSON Schema ownership;
- species-occurrence ownership;
- EvidenceBundle, receipt, proof, or regulatory-record creation;
- release, deployment, publication, correction, withdrawal, or rollback execution;
- API, UI, MapLibre, tile, export, report, or AI implementation;
- regulatory designation, legal conclusion, conservation mandate, or proof of species presence;
- activation by filename, directory presence, default declaration, marker, or documentation assertion.

### Non-goals

This README does not:

1. accept the eighteen Rego files;
2. choose between the current `allow` and `deny` relations;
3. resolve duplicate concepts, package namespaces, contract/schema duplicates, or umbrella vocabulary;
4. activate a child lane, bundle, evaluator, selector, alias, workflow, or consumer;
5. certify adjacent tests, validators, fixtures, contracts, schemas, sources, or workflows;
6. designate critical habitat or establish scientific or legal thresholds;
7. assign functional stewards from CODEOWNERS routing;
8. promote, release, deploy, publish, correct, withdraw, or roll back an artifact.

## Authority level

**Draft directory boundary / proposed policy-source index / no runtime, release, publication, regulatory, or legal authority.**

[ADR-0029](../../../docs/adr/ADR-0029-adopt-directory-governance-standard-v2.md) accepts Directory Rules v2 as repository placement authority. The [root registry](../../../control_plane/root_registry.yaml) assigns the singular `policy/` root normative allow, deny, hold, restrict, and abstain rules and prohibits data instances, release decisions, and schemas here. Those accepted placement rules do not accept this domain's behavior or activate these files.

The [domain lane register](../../../control_plane/domain_lane_register.yaml) lists Habitat in a `PROPOSED`, machine-projection-only inventory and records a T0 baseline. Neither the projection nor that baseline creates domain, policy, or sensitivity authority. Joined occurrence, private-land, stewardship, or otherwise protected context can require a more restrictive output posture.

### Governing order

When artifacts disagree, apply the most recent accepted authority in this order:

1. KFM core invariants and accepted operating law;
2. accepted ADRs and Directory Rules;
3. accepted Habitat semantic, source-role, rights, sensitivity, and public-serving doctrine;
4. accepted contracts, schemas, policy vocabularies, bundles, and evaluator profiles;
5. released artifacts and their correction or rollback records;
6. this README;
7. proposed Rego, examples, fixtures, workflow profiles, draft ADRs, and scaffolds.

A lower-ranked artifact cannot weaken a higher-ranked denial, restriction, attribution, sensitivity, evidence, review, release, correction, or rollback requirement.

## Repository fit and responsibility boundary

The accepted responsibility root is `policy/`. Habitat appears in the proposed machine domain-lane projection, while the domain-policy parent documents the repository's thirteen domain segments. The [Habitat control-plane README](../../../control_plane/domains/habitat/README.md) is a referential governance index, not policy or semantic authority.

~~~text
Habitat semantics/contracts/schemas/source roles
                    │ governed references
                    ▼
governed request + protected context ──► accepted Habitat policy source
                                                   │
                                                   ▼
                                      immutable bundle + evaluator
                                                   │ raw result
                                                   ▼
                                       normalized PolicyDecision
                                                   │ obligations preserved
                                                   ▼
                              governed consumer + separate lifecycle gates
~~~

### Boundary invariants

1. A repository path or marker does not activate policy.
2. Rule source does not own its semantic inputs, sources, or evidence.
3. A default relation is not a normalized decision.
4. Policy evaluation does not admit, promote, release, deploy, publish, or legally designate an object.
5. A passing fixture workflow does not prove Rego enforcement.
6. Public consumers cannot select a repository slug, package, entrypoint, or bundle.
7. Modeled suitability is not regulatory critical habitat.
8. Land cover is not species occurrence.
9. Context joins do not transfer domain ownership.
10. Rights, source role, sensitivity, time, uncertainty, evidence, and correction obligations survive every adapter.
11. Missing or unresolved context cannot be converted into permission.
12. Protected geometry is transformed before public materialization, not hidden only by style.

## What belongs here

After appropriate acceptance and review, this directory may contain:

- Habitat-specific Rego or equivalent normative policy source;
- package and entrypoint documentation;
- rule-local helper modules whose only responsibility is Habitat admissibility;
- policy-native unit tests if the accepted repository layout places them beside source;
- non-secret test data explicitly allowed by the accepted policy-test layout;
- source-to-bundle inclusion metadata that does not itself activate a bundle;
- deprecation, supersession, correction, and rollback pointers for rule source;
- this README and other narrowly scoped policy-source documentation.

Every future executable file must identify:

- accepted policy and semantic authority;
- package namespace and entrypoint;
- exact input and evaluator contracts;
- output relation and normalization behavior;
- failure and missing-input behavior;
- reason and obligation mapping;
- source-role, rights, sensitivity, temporal, uncertainty, and evidence dependencies;
- native tests and bundle inclusion;
- owners, reviewers, activation state, correction, and rollback path.

### What these defaults mean—and do not mean

- `default allow := false` makes that package relation false absent another rule; it does not define a complete decision, reason, obligation, audience, trace, or lifecycle effect.

- A filename containing `deny`, `abstain`, `release_gate`, or a doctrinal phrase has no executable meaning beyond its rule body.
- Comments and example rules are documentation, not active logic.
- Eighteen individually simple defaults do not form a composable policy without an accepted entrypoint and result adapter.

## Habitat policy spine

Future accepted policy must preserve what a habitat claim is, how it was produced, who owns it, who may use it, how sensitive it becomes in context, and what operation is requested.

### Object-family context

The Habitat doctrine identifies eleven canonical object families:

| Object family | Policy-relevant distinction |
|---|---|
| HabitatPatch | Place/landscape unit; not species occurrence |
| LandCoverObservation | Observation with source and time; not an eternal class |
| EcologicalSystem | Classified system; classifier/version must remain explicit |
| Habitat Quality Score | Derived assessment; method, scale, uncertainty, and evidence required |
| SuitabilityModel | Modeled surface; not observation or regulatory designation |
| ConnectivityEdge | Derived graph relationship; assumptions and resistance model required |
| Corridor | Derived connectivity product; not an automatic legal or stewardship boundary |
| Restoration Opportunity | Candidate assessment; not approval, funding, mandate, or completed restoration |
| StewardshipZone | Management context; not ownership, access permission, or regulatory designation |
| Model Run Receipt | Reproducibility record; not evidence closure, approval, or release |
| UncertaintySurface | Explicit modeled uncertainty; must travel with the governed product |

This list is semantic context, not an input schema. An evaluator must consume accepted versioned identifiers and shapes rather than parse prose or infer an object family from a path.

### Source roles

Habitat policy must preserve these roles where applicable:

- `observed`;
- `regulatory`;
- `modeled`;
- `aggregate`;
- `administrative`;
- `candidate`;
- `synthetic`.

Role is not a quality score. A high-quality model remains modeled; a regulatory record remains regulatory only when its source, jurisdiction, effective state, and citation are intact; an aggregate cannot be reverse-labeled as an observation.

### Anti-collapse rules

| Boundary | Required posture |
|---|---|
| Modeled suitability vs regulatory critical habitat | Never label or query a model output as an official designation |
| Regulatory critical habitat vs species presence | A designation is not an occurrence record or proof of current presence |
| Land cover vs species occurrence | Habitat owns place/context; Fauna or Flora owns occurrence truth |
| Model vs observation | Preserve source role, method, uncertainty, and temporal scope |
| Class vs classifier | Preserve scheme, version, crosswalk direction, and lossiness |
| Quality score vs fact | Preserve method, scale, limitations, and evidence |
| Connectivity/corridor vs mandate | Derived landscape structure is not legal access, ownership, or required action |
| Restoration opportunity vs approved project | Candidate status is not authorization, funding, or implementation |
| Stewardship zone vs ownership | Management context does not confer property or access rights |
| Map layer vs evidence | Tiles, rasters, symbols, and styles are carriers, not authority |
| Context join vs canonical claim | A join may support a decision without transferring object ownership |

### Regulatory critical habitat boundary

KFM may represent a cited regulatory record or public-safe derivative when an accepted source, contract, rights posture, effective state, geometry treatment, and release record allow it. KFM does not create the designation.

An accepted rule must deny or abstain when:

- a modeled product is labeled as regulatory;
- a regulatory source, jurisdiction, version, or effective date is missing;
- the requested interpretation exceeds the source record;
- a designation is used as proof of species presence;
- protected detail would be exposed by the requested output;
- legal or conservation advice is requested beyond KFM's approved response boundary.

### Sensitivity and geoprivacy

Sensitivity is a property of the proposed output in context, not merely of an input file. Habitat context may become restricted when joined with animal occurrence, rare-plant records, private-land detail, precise stewardship information, unpublished observations, or other protected data.

Required invariants:

1. Sensitive joins fail closed when classification, purpose, audience, transform, review, or evidence is unresolved.
2. Public-safe generalization, aggregation, displacement, redaction, omission, or denial occurs before tile or artifact generation.
3. A style filter, hidden layer, client-side toggle, zoom threshold, or undocumented aggregation is not a security boundary.
4. A transform must be reproducible and receipted without preserving protected coordinates in public logs or metadata.
5. Public derivatives cannot be reverse-expanded or joined back to reveal protected detail.
6. AI, search, export, download, report, and map routes enforce the same boundary.
7. The most restrictive applicable rights and sensitivity obligations survive caching, correction, and supersession.

The current [habitat sensitivity YAML](../../sensitivity/habitat_classes.yaml) is only a proposed placeholder pointing to a missing-or-planned document. It does not implement these rules.

### Cross-domain ownership

| Join | Habitat contribution | Other authority | Required guard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Habitat ↔ Fauna | Patch, cover, modeled suitability, connectivity | Animal occurrence | Geoprivacy, purpose, audience, ownership, and output transform |
| Habitat ↔ Flora | Ecological system, cover, patch context | Plant/taxon/specimen and rare-record truth | Rare-record sensitivity and ownership preservation |
| Habitat ↔ Hydrology | Riparian, wetland, moisture, and flow context | Hydrologic observations/models | Version, time, uncertainty, and pair-policy boundary |
| Habitat ↔ Soil | Substrate and soil context | Soil object authority | Source-role and temporal alignment |
| Habitat ↔ Hazards | Stress and disturbance context | Hazard authority | Do not convert context into impact certainty |
| Habitat ↔ Agriculture | Land-use and management context | Agriculture authority | Rights, time, purpose, and ownership preservation |

Pair-policy documentation under [Habitat–Fauna](../../joins/habitat-fauna/README.md) and [Habitat–Hydrology](../../joins/habitat-hydrology/README.md) remains draft and evaluator-unbound. It must be composed explicitly; directory proximity does not activate it.

## Decision normalization boundary

The present packages do not share one result relation. A future adapter must never treat false uniformly.

### Candidate finite outcomes

The broader KFM doctrine describes candidate consumer-facing outcomes such as:

| Outcome | Meaning at the policy boundary | Not implied |
|---|---|---|
| `ANSWER` | Policy produced a governed result for the declared operation | Release or ecological correctness |
| `ABSTAIN` | Policy intentionally provides no substantive answer | Permission |
| `DENY` | The requested operation is prohibited under the evaluated policy | Deletion or punishment |
| `ERROR` | Evaluation could not complete reliably | Allow, deny, or abstain |

These labels are not accepted Habitat runtime vocabulary. They are a candidate normalization target until an accepted contract binds raw package relations, reasons, obligations, and consumers.

### Required distinctions

- `allow == false` is not automatically `DENY`.
- `deny == false` is not automatically `ANSWER` or permission.
- no result is not automatically `ABSTAIN`.
- invalid input is not a policy denial unless the accepted contract says so.
- evaluator failure is not an ecological or regulatory conclusion.
- a policy answer is not lifecycle promotion, review approval, or publication.

### Normalization requirements

1. Reject unknown packages, entrypoints, relation shapes, reasons, and obligations.
2. Reject contradictory raw results.
3. Distinguish intentional abstention from missing implementation and evaluator error.
4. Bind every result to exact input, bundle, evaluator, and adapter identities.
5. Preserve sensitivity, transform, attribution, limitation, correction, and non-publication obligations.
6. Prevent downstream consumers from weakening or silently dropping obligations.
7. Emit no protected coordinates, private payloads, or sensitive join details in public traces.

## Release, correction, and rollback

### Lifecycle separation

Policy evaluation can constrain a lifecycle action; it does not perform it. A file named `release_gate.rego` does not approve a release. A release candidate, review record, manifest, public-safe transform receipt, and publisher boundary remain separate.

### Correction triggers

A correction or withdrawal assessment is required when:

- a source, rights term, regulatory record, classifier, model, or crosswalk is corrected or withdrawn;
- a model/observation, modeled/regulatory, land-cover/occurrence, or domain-ownership collapse is found;
- geoprivacy or join-induced sensitivity was misclassified;
- protected detail reached a derivative, cache, tile, export, report, log, or AI response;
- a bundle, evaluator, adapter, reason, or obligation mapping was wrong;
- a public consumer dropped an obligation;
- the evidence, review, or release chain was incomplete.

### Runtime rollback record

An executable rollback needs:

- incident/correction identifier;
- exact bad binding and affected consumers;
- exact prior bundle, evaluator, adapter, selector, and configuration digests;
- authorization and separation-of-duties record;
- cache, tile, export, index, and AI invalidation scope;
- post-rollback validation and monitoring;
- protected-detail containment evidence;
- forward-fix and reactivation criteria.

Rollback must not mean recursively selecting whatever older files remain in the directory.

### README rollback

The previous target blob is pinned in metadata. Reverting documentation does not revert a rule, bundle, evaluator, release, cache, or public artifact. Runtime and release rollback require their own exact-target records.

## Smallest sound resolution sequence

1. **Freeze activation.** Keep all eighteen files and child markers out of active bundle selection.
2. **Confirm ownership.** Record Habitat, policy, source, rights, sensitivity, runtime, release, and consumer stewards.
3. **Inventory consumers.** Find tracked and deployed references to paths, packages, rule names, schemas, and reason codes.
4. **Accept semantics.** Resolve object/source roles, model/observation, modeled/regulatory, occurrence ownership, and Biotopes compatibility.
5. **Resolve identities.** Choose canonical contract/schema identities and preserve supersession history.
6. **Define one interface.** Accept input, package, entrypoint, raw result, normalized outcome, reasons, and obligations.
7. **Implement one thin slice.** Prefer a bounded no-network rule family with complete positive and negative tests.
8. **Prove geoprivacy.** Test protected joins and pre-materialization transforms end to end.
9. **Bind immutably.** Create an explicit manifest, bundle digest, evaluator/adapter compatibility record, and selector.
10. **Review independently.** Complete ecological, source-rights, sensitivity, security, runtime, and release review.
11. **Activate narrowly.** Record environment, audience, operation, effective time, monitoring, and exact rollback target.
12. **Release separately.** Require evidence, review, public-safe transformation, release manifest, correction, and rollback gates.

Each step is independently reviewable and reversible. No later step is implied by completion of an earlier one.
