## Purpose

This directory owns the **Settlements & Infrastructure-specific portion of KFM admissibility-policy source and its local boundary documentation**. Its eventual role is to answer bounded questions such as:

- whether a settlement, municipality, census place, historic townsite, infrastructure asset, network, facility, service area, operator, condition observation, dependency, map layer, export, or AI-supported answer has enough governed context for the requested operation;
- whether legal-source role, EvidenceBundle resolution, rights, sensitivity, temporal validity, lifecycle state, review, release, correction, and rollback prerequisites are satisfied;
- whether exact geometry, interior detail, condition, topology, dependencies, operator information, cultural or sovereignty-bearing context, or private joins must be generalized, redacted, withheld, quarantined, or sent to specialist review;
- whether census, legal-municipality, historic-place, colloquial-place, facility, and operator roles are being collapsed into a false single identity;
- whether a derived graph, tile, footprint, service area, or generated narrative is being mistaken for canonical evidence; and
- whether a finite denial, abstention, hold, restriction, or error is required instead of an affirmative result.

The current files do **not** implement that complete behavior. This README records the boundary and the gap without upgrading scaffolds to active policy.

## Required policy input boundary

No direct rule should be treated as safe to evaluate until an accepted operation-specific input profile binds at least:

| Input family | Required context |
|---|---|
| Operation | Exact action such as answer, render, inspect, join, export, transform, promote, release, correct, withdraw, or roll back |
| Actor and audience | Authenticated caller, capability, purpose, audience, and scope; absence is not public permission |
| Object identity | Stable object family and role, domain identity, source IDs, aliases, merge/split/supersession lineage, and relevant geometry reference |
| Role separation | Legal municipality, census place, historic townsite, settlement, facility, operator, condition, service area, and dependency roles remain distinct |
| Source descriptors | Source identity, authority role, rights, license, access, cadence, retrieval state, limitations, and citation requirements |
| Evidence | Exact EvidenceRefs and resolution state, claim coverage, source-role sufficiency, and unresolved conflicts |
| Rights and consent | Permitted use, redistribution, attribution, privacy, community or sovereignty constraints, and any revocation |
| Sensitivity | Classification, exact-harm and reconstruction risk, critical-asset detail, private joins, cultural restrictions, and accepted transform profile |
| Time | Source, observed, valid, retrieval, release, correction, expiry, and supersession time where material |
| Lifecycle and release | Current lifecycle state, immutable artifact identity, review state, release decision, correction state, and rollback target |
| Policy execution | Exact source or bundle ID, version, digest, selector, evaluator, entrypoint, input hash, and effective time |
| Cross-domain joins | Roads/rail, hazards, hydrology, people/land, archaeology/cultural, graph, spatial, and other relevant rule results without ownership collapse |
| Consumer capability | Whether the API, map, export, graph, or AI consumer can enforce every surviving obligation and visible negative state |

### Input invariants

- Missing or untrusted required context never becomes implicit permission.
- A public source is not automatically authoritative for legal status, current condition, service availability, dependency, or safe public precision.
- A source admitted for one role must not be silently upcast to another.
- Census geography, legal municipal status, historic identity, colloquial naming, and facility identity must not collapse.
- Old evidence must not silently support a current-state claim; time semantics remain explicit.
- A source, schema, EvidenceRef, fixture, workflow, or map layer cannot self-approve its use.
- Policy evaluation must not perform hidden network fetches to fill missing inputs.
- Cross-domain composition inherits the most restrictive applicable obligation and may become more sensitive through linkage.

## Outputs and authority limits

An accepted future evaluator may emit an engine-native result that is normalized into a closed outward decision object. The outward result must preserve, rather than blur:

| Result | Meaning | Never means |
|---|---|---|
| `ANSWER` | The bounded operation may proceed under the exact evaluated context and enforceable obligations | The underlying claim is globally true, current, safe, legally authoritative, released, or permanently public |
| `ABSTAIN` | Evidence or required context is insufficient or unresolved for an affirmative answer | Permission to guess, interpolate, infer a status, or substitute generated prose |
| `DENY` | Policy prohibits the operation, precision, audience, use, or release | A factual finding about the object or permission to expose internal reasons |
| `ERROR` | Evaluation or obligation enforcement could not complete safely | A fallback to allow or an excuse to return unfiltered data |

Internal workflow or review states such as `HOLD`, `RESTRICT`, `QUARANTINE`, or `REVIEW_REQUIRED` must map through an accepted contract without collapsing outward `ABSTAIN`, `DENY`, and `ERROR`.

Every affirmative result should be bound to policy and evaluator identity, input and artifact hashes, source/evidence references, safe reasons, obligations, decision time, expiry or re-evaluation trigger, review state, and correction/rollback lineage. Those decision instances do not belong in this source directory.

## Public-surface and governed-AI boundary

### Governed API, map, graph, and export

- Public clients consume released public-safe artifacts through governed interfaces, never source registries, policy files, proofs, or candidate lanes directly.

If required evidence or the policy path is unavailable, the correct answer is a visible limitation or abstention—not a plausible place or infrastructure narrative.
