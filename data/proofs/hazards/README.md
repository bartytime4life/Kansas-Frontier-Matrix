## Purpose

`data/proofs/hazards/` is the Hazards domain proof-support lane under the canonical `data/proofs/` responsibility. It supports review of historical hazard events, observations, remote-sensing detections, models, forecasts, warning or advisory context, regulatory hazard areas, disaster declarations, exposure summaries, resilience summaries, timelines, maps, reports, and other derived carriers.

The lane exists to make bounded questions inspectable before a claim advances:

1. What exact hazard claim or artifact scope is being supported?
2. Which admitted sources, source roles, and evidence records support it?
3. Which event, issue, valid, expiry, retrieval, processing, release, correction, and stale-state times govern interpretation?
4. Which rights, sensitivity, public-safe geometry, official-referral, and not-for-life-safety controls apply?
5. Which validators, receipts, policy decisions, reviews, catalogs, release dependencies, corrections, withdrawals, and rollback targets remain outside the proof packet?
6. Which finite downstream outcome—answer, abstain, hold, restrict, deny, or error—is justified?

This lane supports evidence and release review. It does not admit sources, determine current conditions, issue alerts, decide policy, approve release, publish maps, or authorize emergency or regulatory action.

## Hazards claim and source-role closure

Hazards proofs must preserve the object and source role that each claim can actually support.

| Claim or carrier | Required distinction | Must not become |
|---|---|---|
| `HazardEvent` | Historical or observed event identity, source, time, space, uncertainty | Current warning or regulatory determination |
| `HazardObservation` | Observation versus model, station/sensor role, quality and retrieval state | Forecast or event truth without support |
| `WarningContext` / `AdvisoryContext` | Official issuer, product class, issue/valid/expiry times, stale state, official referral | KFM warning, alert, or instruction |
| `DisasterDeclaration` | Administrative/legal authority, jurisdiction, effective dates | Physical hazard observation by itself |
| `FloodContext` | Regulatory NFHL context, hydrologic observation/model context, effective date | Observed inundation, forecast, or KFM regulatory determination |
| `WildfireDetection` / `RemoteSensingDetection` | Detection role, processing level, confidence, temporal lag | Confirmed incident extent or response guidance |
| `SmokeContext` | Observation versus model, Atmosphere/Air ownership, time and health-advice boundary | KFM health instruction or Hazards-owned air truth |
| `DroughtIndicator` | Indicator method, class/version, aggregation scale, time window | Farm-level condition or forecast without evidence |
| `EarthquakeEvent` | Official event identity, magnitude/depth uncertainty, revision state | Engineering or emergency guidance |
| `ExposureSummary` / `ImpactArea` | Cross-lane joins, public-safe aggregation, uncertainty, infrastructure sensitivity | Exact critical-asset inventory or operational vulnerability map |
| `ResilienceSummary` | Assumptions, scenario and planning limits, source roles, review state | Guarantee, official plan, or policy decision |
| Model, forecast, aggregate, candidate, or synthetic carrier | Explicit product class and limitations | Observation merely because it is mapped or summarized |

Cross-lane evidence remains owned by its source domain. Hydrology, Atmosphere/Air, Settlements/Infrastructure, Roads/Rail, Agriculture, Geology, Health, and other lanes do not become Hazards truth through a join.

## Life-safety, official-referral, and authority boundary

The following conditions are mandatory for any proof packet touching operational products or urgent-use interpretation:

- KFM remains planning, history, evidence, and resilience context only;
- the official issuer and source URL or identifier remain visible;
- issue, valid, expiry, retrieval, and stale-state times remain visible;
- expired or stale context cannot be presented as current;
- the output carries a not-for-life-safety posture in machine and human-readable form;
- urgent-use surfaces refer users to the applicable official emergency, weather, geological, fire, health, tribal, state, county, or local authority;
- generated language cannot add emergency or regulatory instructions not contained in released evidence;
- absence of official-referral, time validity, or policy support yields `DENY`, `ABSTAIN`, `HOLD`, or `ERROR`.

No proof file, workflow, green check, map layer, dashboard, AI answer, or release candidate can convert KFM into an alert, warning, evacuation, rescue, emergency, legal, or regulatory authority.

## Temporal, freshness, expiry, and stale state

Hazards interpretation is time-sensitive. Keep these time dimensions separate where material:

| Time field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Event or observation time | When the hazard event or observation occurred |
| Issue time | When an official product was issued |
| Valid interval | When the product claims applicability |
| Expiry time | When an operational product ceases to be current |
| Source update time | When the upstream source revised or replaced the record |
| Retrieval time | When KFM acquired or checked the source |
| Processing time | When KFM transformed or validated the record |
| Review time | When the packet was reviewed |
| Release time | When a governed public state began |
| Correction or withdrawal time | When prior support or public state changed |

A proof packet must state the freshness profile and stale behavior. Missing or incoherent temporal support blocks current-language presentation. Historical claims may remain valid after operational expiry only when they are clearly reclassified as historical context and the supporting evidence remains released and non-withdrawn.

## Spatial sensitivity and public-safe representation

Hazard data can become sensitive through precision or joins even when individual inputs are public. Review for:

- exact critical infrastructure, facility, utility, communications, transportation, emergency-operation, response-capability, health, shelter, private-property, or living-person detail;
- re-identifying combinations across Hazards and adjacent domains;
- exact impact, vulnerability, resource, or response maps that increase misuse risk;
- source terms or sovereignty constraints requiring restricted handling;
- public-safe generalization that could still be reversed through labels, timestamps, or companion layers.

Use aggregation, generalization, suppression, redaction, delayed publication, staged access, quarantine, or denial as policy requires. Record the transform and reason in the appropriate receipt or decision object. A styled-hidden feature is not a safe transform.

## Correction, withdrawal, invalidation, and rollback

A proof record must not be silently edited to erase a material error or changed safety posture.

Correction or invalidation is required when:

- evidence is wrong, missing, stale, withdrawn, superseded, or mis-cited;
- source role, product class, authority, rights, or sensitivity was mislabeled;
- an expired product was represented as current;
- KFM wording implied warning, alert, regulatory, emergency, or life-safety authority;
- a public-safe transform leaks or permits reconstruction of sensitive detail;
- catalog, proof, receipt, candidate, release, or digest identity no longer agrees;
- a correction, withdrawal, or supersession changes the supported claim scope.

Preserve prior identifiers and public correction lineage. Propagate invalidation to dependent catalogs, triplets, candidates, releases, API payloads, layers, tiles, exports, search indexes, graphs, summaries, caches, Evidence Drawer payloads, and governed-AI outputs as applicable.

Future proof implementation must define rollback for stale or wrong source support, incorrect temporal state, source-role correction, rights or sensitivity changes, unsafe public-safe transforms, proof invalidation propagation, withdrawn candidates or releases, public artifact/cache invalidation, and restoration of the last known governed state without deleting correction history.
