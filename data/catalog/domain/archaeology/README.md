## Purpose

`data/catalog/domain/archaeology/` owns governed Archaeology and Cultural Heritage catalog descriptors and indexes at the `CATALOG / TRIPLET` lifecycle stage. Records here connect stable object or product identities to bounded source roles, evidence, receipts, review posture, sensitivity transformations, policy state, catalog projections, release state, correction lineage, and rollback targets.

Likely record families include survey projects and transects, site candidates and components, shovel tests and excavation units, provenience and stratigraphic contexts, artifact and collection-repository records, chronology assertions, candidate features, geophysics observations, remote-sensing anomalies, LiDAR candidates, 3D documentation, and public-safe generalized products. Listing a family does not prove that its records, contract, schema, validator, evidence, cultural authorization, or release state exist.

## Authority level

**Canonical responsibility placement for Archaeology domain catalog records / repository-grounded draft / concrete profile and realization PROPOSED / not source, truth, cultural-authority, evidence, policy, release, or publication authority.**

## Inputs

Catalog construction may consume references to the following governed inputs; it does not take ownership of them:

- admitted `PROCESSED` Archaeology objects or public-safe product candidates;
- stable object, product, version, algorithm-qualified digest, spatial scope, temporal scope, and lifecycle identity;
- `SourceDescriptor` records with source role, rights, access, retrieval context, and limitations;
- `EvidenceRef -> EvidenceBundle` support for claim-bearing fields;
- sensitivity classification, reconstruction-risk assessment, and reviewed transform receipts where applicable;
- safe cultural, sovereignty, rights-holder, steward, and institutional review references and finite outcomes;
- accepted contract, schema/profile, vocabulary, identity, and compatibility rules;
- validation reports, process receipts, policy decisions, release-candidate state, correction lineage, and rollback targets appropriate to intended use.

Missing, stale, conflicted, revoked, inaccessible, unauthorized, or insufficient inputs remain visible as `HOLD`, `ABSTAIN`, `DENY`, `RESTRICT`, `ERROR`, `UNKNOWN`, or `NEEDS VERIFICATION`; they do not become implied success.

## Outputs

This lane may emit or maintain:

- governed Archaeology domain catalog records and deterministic indexes;
- safe cross-references to applicable STAC, DCAT, PROV, triplet, evidence, policy, review, and release projections;
- release-candidate catalog subsets that remain non-public until an immutable release decision closes;
- public-review-safe status and quality summaries that point to authoritative records;
- correction, withdrawal, supersession, and dependency references needed to prevent stale discovery;
- inspectable catalog build and validation references without collapsing receipt, proof, policy, review, or release authority.

It emits no source observation, cultural decision, policy permission, release approval, runtime response, public route, or published artifact by placement alone.

## Sensitivity guardrails

1. **Sensitive precision fails closed.** Exact or reverse-engineerable site geometry, burial and human-remains context, sacred or culturally restricted material, collection-security details, looting-risk information, and private-landowner data must not enter public-readable catalog surfaces.
2. **Catalog metadata cannot leak more than the carrier.** Titles, descriptions, identifiers, bounding boxes, temporal clues, attributes, related-record links, thumbnails, tiles, indexes, search, and generated summaries require the same reconstruction-risk review as geometry.
3. **Cultural authority remains separate.** Rights, sovereignty, cultural review, steward authority, consent, institutional custody, policy, and public-release review are distinct gates. Possession, documentation, schema validity, or catalog presence satisfies none of them.
4. **Candidate is not confirmed.** Remote-sensing anomalies, geophysics observations, LiDAR candidates, candidate features, and unverified reports retain candidate or observation state until evidence and appropriate review support a narrower claim.
5. **Absence is bounded.** Survey coverage or a catalog gap does not prove the absence of a site, cultural significance, or protected knowledge.
6. **Source roles do not collapse.** Survey, excavation, collection, oral-history, cultural-knowledge, remote-sensing, archival, registry, and modeled sources retain distinct provenance, limits, time, rights, and authority.
7. **Generalized output is a derivative.** Redaction, suppression, buffering, aggregation, generalization, withholding, or delayed release preserves input/output identity, method/profile, information loss, reason, reviewer, digest, obligations, correction, and rollback lineage.
8. **Public status elsewhere is not KFM permission.** An externally visible record does not automatically resolve source terms, cultural authority, landowner sensitivity, human-remains obligations, re-identification risk, or KFM release review.
9. **Protected reasons remain safe.** Public denial, hold, or abstention messages use bounded reason codes and never reveal the protected fact they are withholding.
10. **AI remains interpretive.** Generated language, classification, reconstruction, translation, or site suggestion cannot become evidence, cultural interpretation, confirmation, or approval. Resolve governed support and policy first—or narrow and abstain.

## Projection and release closure

```text
domain record validity
  != STAC / DCAT / PROV agreement
  != evidence closure
  != cultural or rights-holder authorization
  != sensitivity-transform approval
  != policy permission
  != release approval
  != publication
```

When multiple projections exist, they should agree on the same effective object or released derivative, algorithm-qualified digest, release reference, source role, candidate/confirmed state, spatial and temporal scope, rights/access posture, sensitivity transforms, review status, and correction state. A `CatalogMatrix` may describe that agreement; a separate validation report proves what was checked; separate review and policy records decide admissibility; a separate release decision authorizes exposure.

Identity, digest, release-reference, candidate-state, evidence, rights, sovereignty, sensitivity, protected-precision, review, restricted-public, withdrawn, and correction contradictions fail closed for public-bound records. There is no documentation-only, client-hiding, or warn-only bypass for release-significant disagreement.

1. Freeze the target ref, blobs, catalog profile, record inventory, writers, consumers, projections, releases, and public surfaces without copying protected payloads into review artifacts.

## Definition of done

This README upgrade is complete when the document passes source validation and accountable human review. The Archaeology catalog lane itself is operationally complete only when:

- actual records, indexes, writers, consumers, hosts, caches, projections, and public effects are safely inventoried;
- the Archaeology domain catalog contract, schema/profile, compatibility policy, producers, synthetic fixtures, validators, and required checks are accepted and executable;
- every claim-bearing record resolves source role, evidence, rights, sovereignty, sensitivity, transforms, review, policy, release, correction, expiry/revocation, and rollback appropriate to its significance and audience;
- exact or reverse-engineerable location and protected cultural substance cannot enter public-readable records, logs, fixtures, indexes, maps, media, caches, exports, or generated language;
- candidate features and observations cannot be presented as confirmed sites, cultural interpretation, or public authorization;
- domain, STAC, DCAT, PROV, triplet, evidence, review, policy, and release views agree without collapsing their authority roles;
- candidate, restricted, stale, withdrawn, superseded, revoked, expired, or unreleased records cannot reach public clients;
- public consumers use governed interfaces and released public-safe artifacts, not canonical catalog storage;
- correction, withdrawal, supersession, invalidation, and rollback are tested without rewriting history or exposing protected details.

A polished README, passing parser, green readiness workflow, valid schema, or catalog record alone does not establish these conditions.
