## Purpose

`data/catalog/domain/people/dna/` is a repository-present compatibility sublane inside the `CATALOG / TRIPLET` lifecycle stage. Its bounded purpose is to explain or, after a reviewed path decision, index DNA-related catalog metadata that must remain subordinate to the People/DNA/Land domain and its deny-by-default controls.

Until the path conflict is resolved, the safest accepted role is documentation, migration metadata, compatibility crosswalks, and release-linked pointers that resolve back to governed objects in their owning roots. A concrete DNA catalog record family, active producer, active consumer, public route, and retained short-segment profile are **not established** by the inspected evidence.

A catalog entry may support discovery, review, catalog closure, correction tracing, and release preparation. It does **not**:

- establish person identity, biological relationship, kinship, ancestry, or consent;
- upgrade DNA evidence into a canonical relationship;
- make restricted material public-safe;
- activate a source, schema, policy, validator, or pipeline;
- authorize release or publication; or
- replace evidence, proof, receipt, review, correction, withdrawal, or rollback objects.

## Authority level

**Compatibility / transitional sublane within the canonical `data/catalog/` responsibility root; repository-present; segment authority conflicted; no independent truth, consent, policy, release, or publication authority.**

Directory Rules place domain catalog material under `data/catalog/domain/<domain>/` and explicitly name `people-dna-land` as the domain segment. The repository also contains the short-segment compatibility parent `data/catalog/domain/people/` and this `dna/` child. Those facts establish repository presence, not canonical promotion.

## Inputs

Inputs are catalog-stage metadata and stable references derived from governed upstream work. They may be considered only after the applicable source, evidence, identity, time, rights, consent, revocation, sensitivity, validation, policy, review, and lifecycle conditions are resolved.

Expected input classes, if this compatibility lane is retained, include:

- deterministic object and catalog identifiers;
- source and source-role references;
- evidence references that resolve to EvidenceBundles;
- contract and schema version references;
- privacy and sensitivity classifications;
- consent and revocation references for the exact operation, audience, purpose, field or relation, precision, and time window;
- transformation, aggregation, redaction, or public-safe-derivative receipts;
- validation and review references;
- immutable artifact and release references;
- correction, withdrawal, supersession, and rollback references; and
- compatibility or migration mapping from the short segment to the accepted domain identity.

Raw DNA, direct identifiers, credentials, secrets, unresolved rights material, and public-facing bytes are never inputs to this README lane.

## Outputs

Permitted outputs are internal compatibility metadata, catalog indexes, migration crosswalks, and release-linked pointers that preserve the authoritative objects in their owning roots.

An output from this lane is not:

- a DNA, identity, relationship, ancestry, or consent finding;
- an EvidenceBundle or proof;
- a PolicyDecision, ReviewRecord, or ReleaseManifest;
- a released artifact;
- a public endpoint; or
- permission for a client, model, map, or analyst to read an internal store directly.

Ordinary public clients may consume only governed API responses or immutable, release-resolved public-safe carriers. They must not traverse this catalog directory, source registries, consent internals, proofs, receipts, candidates, or canonical stores.

<a id="dna-guardrails"></a>

## DNA anti-collapse and exposure rules

1. **DNA evidence is not identity or kinship truth.** A match or segment may support a `RelationshipHypothesis`; it does not become a confirmed relationship through cataloging.
2. **A catalog record is not evidence, policy, review, release, or publication.** Each remains a separate governed object.
3. **Consent does not publish data.** Consent is operation-specific and independent of evidence, rights, sensitivity, review, release, correction, and rollback gates.
4. **Tokenization and pseudonymization do not make data anonymous or public.** Linkage and reconstruction risk remain review obligations.
5. **Raw genotype, raw segments, kit/vendor IDs, and individual match tables never become public catalog material.**
6. **Living-person ambiguity fails closed.** Unknown or disputed living status cannot become implicit public permission.
7. **A DNA-only relationship inference remains a hypothesis.** Catalog metadata must preserve uncertainty, source role, evidence diversity, and review state.
8. **Aggregation is not a universal allow path.** K-anonymity, differential privacy, generalization, and redaction require accepted profiles, receipts, reconstruction-risk review, and release closure.
9. **A neighboring domain may supply context, not weaker controls.** Geography, settlement, parcel, land, archaeology, agriculture, or other joins inherit the most restrictive applicable policy.
10. **Public clients never read this lane directly.** UI hiding, style filtering, private-looking URLs, or model refusal prompts cannot repair an unsafe delivered payload.
11. **Missing or stale support narrows or denies use.** Missing evidence, rights, consent, revocation state, sensitivity, review, correction, release, or rollback never becomes a plausible default.
12. **Generated language is interpretive only.** An AI summary cannot create DNA evidence, consent, policy approval, relationship truth, or release state.

## Consent, revocation, correction, and rollback

Consent must be explicit, machine-verifiable, immutable as an event, revocable, and bounded to the precise use under evaluation. A valid grant for one source, purpose, audience, field, relation, precision, derivative, export, or time window does not authorize another.

Fail closed when consent is missing, stale, unverifiable, expired, suspended, revoked, disputed, ambiguous, broader than the requested use, or affected by another person's rights.

Revocation or later-discovered risk requires the applicable:

1. deny or hold at the access and render gate;
2. immutable revocation or supersession event;
3. downstream dependency inventory;
4. cache, tile, search, graph, vector, API, model-context, and static-carrier invalidation;
5. correction, withdrawal, or tombstone record;
6. release-state update;
7. public notice where required;
8. revalidation of remaining derivatives; and
9. rollback to the last known-safe release or complete withdrawal.

The repository documents this intent, but the inspected evidence does not establish deployed revocation introspection, receipt emission, dependency tracing, cache invalidation, or rollback automation. Treat those capabilities as **NOT ESTABLISHED**, not implied.
